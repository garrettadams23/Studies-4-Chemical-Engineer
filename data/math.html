📐 ODEs & Linear Algebra for Engineers FOUNDATION ▶
The Language of Models
Balances become differential equations
Every dynamic balance — a reactor, a tank, a controller — is a differential equation. Steady multi-unit balances become linear systems. Fluency here is non-negotiable.
▸ FIRST-ORDER LINEAR ODE
dy/dx + P(x) y = Q(x)

Integrating factor:  μ = exp(∫P dx)
Solution:  y = (1/μ) ∫ μ Q dx + C/μ

Classic result: mixing-tank concentration approaches
steady state exponentially with time constant τ = V/Q.
▸ SOLVING LINEAR SYSTEMS A x = b
MethodUse
Gaussian eliminationDirect, dense systems
LU decompositionReuse for many b vectors
EigenvaluesStability, vibration, PCA
🎛️ The eigenvalues of a linearized system's matrix determine stability — negative real parts mean the process returns to steady state.

▸ INTEGRATING ODEs NUMERICALLY
Euler:   y_{n+1} = y_n + h f(x_n, y_n)     (simple, low order)
RK4:     4th-order Runge–Kutta            (workhorse accuracy)

h = step size;  smaller h ⇒ more accurate, more cost.
🐍 A PFR design integral ∫dX/(−r_A) with messy kinetics is exactly the kind of problem Simpson's rule or scipy's solve_ivp handles in seconds.

🔤 Laplace Transforms ODE SOLVER ▶
Turning Calculus into Algebra
The engineer's shortcut for linear ODEs
The Laplace transform converts differential equations into algebraic ones in the variable s, solves them by algebra, then inverts back to time — the mathematical engine of process dynamics and control.
▸ TRANSFORM PAIRS
L{f(t)} = ∫₀^∞ f(t) e^(−st) dt

f(t)          F(s)
1             1/s
e^(−at)       1/(s+a)
sin(ωt)       ω/(s²+ω²)
f'(t)         sF(s) − f(0)
▸ SOLVING WITH LAPLACE
StepAction
1. TransformODE → algebraic equation in s
2. SolveAlgebra for Y(s)
3. InvertPartial fractions → y(t)
🎛️ The transfer function G(s) = Y(s)/U(s) is nothing but the Laplace-domain description of a process — the bridge to the Process Control domain.
🌊 Partial Differential Equations DISTRIBUTED ▶
When Space and Time Both Matter
Transport phenomena speak in PDEs
Whenever a quantity varies in more than one dimension — a temperature profile evolving in time, diffusion into a slab — the model is a partial differential equation.
▸ THE THREE CLASSIC PDEs
TypeEquationPhysics
Parabolic∂u/∂t = α ∇²uHeat / diffusion (transient)
Elliptic∇²u = 0Steady conduction (Laplace)
Hyperbolic∂²u/∂t² = c² ∇²uWaves / convection
▸ SOLUTION ROUTES
Analytical:  separation of variables → Fourier series
Numerical:   finite difference / finite element / finite volume
             (what every CFD & simulation package does)
🔬 The unsteady diffusion/heat equation ∂u/∂t = α ∂²u/∂x² recurs across the whole curriculum — transient conduction, drug release, and Fick's second law are all the same PDE.
