📐 ODEs & Linear Algebra for Engineers FOUNDATION ▶
The Language of Models
Balances become differential equations
Every dynamic balance — a reactor, a tank, a controller — is a differential equation. Steady multi-unit balances become linear systems. Fluency here is non-negotiable.
▸ FIRST-ORDER LINEAR ODE
dy/dx + P(x) y = Q(x)

Integrating factor:  μ = exp(∫P dx)
Solution:  y = (1/μ) ∫ μ Q dx + C/μ

Classic result: mixing-tank concentration approaches
steady state exponentially with time constant τ = V/Q.
▸ SOLVING LINEAR SYSTEMS A x = b
MethodUse
Gaussian eliminationDirect, dense systems
LU decompositionReuse for many b vectors
EigenvaluesStability, vibration, PCA
🎛️ The eigenvalues of a linearized system's matrix determine stability — negative real parts mean the process returns to steady state.

▸ INTEGRATING ODEs NUMERICALLY
Euler:   y_{n+1} = y_n + h f(x_n, y_n)     (simple, low order)
RK4:     4th-order Runge–Kutta            (workhorse accuracy)

h = step size;  smaller h ⇒ more accurate, more cost.

🔤 Laplace Transforms ODE SOLVER ▶
Turning Calculus into Algebra
The engineer's shortcut for linear ODEs
The Laplace transform converts differential equations into algebraic ones in the variable s, solves them by algebra, then inverts back to time — the mathematical engine of process dynamics and control.
▸ TRANSFORM PAIRS
L{f(t)} = ∫₀^∞ f(t) e^(−st) dt

f(t)          F(s)
1             1/s
e^(−at)       1/(s+a)
sin(ωt)       ω/(s²+ω²)
f'(t)         sF(s) − f(0)
▸ SOLVING WITH LAPLACE
StepAction
1. TransformODE → algebraic equation in s
2. SolveAlgebra for Y(s)
3. InvertPartial fractions → y(t)
🎛️ The transfer function G(s) = Y(s)/U(s) is nothing but the Laplace-domain description of a process — the bridge to the Process Control domain.
🌊 Partial Differential Equations DISTRIBUTED ▶
When Space and Time Both Matter
Transport phenomena speak in PDEs
Whenever a quantity varies in more than one dimension — a temperature profile evolving in time, diffusion into a slab — the model is a partial differential equation.
▸ THE THREE CLASSIC PDEs
TypeEquationPhysics
Parabolic∂u/∂t = α ∇²uHeat / diffusion (transient)
Elliptic∇²u = 0Steady conduction (Laplace)
Hyperbolic∂²u/∂t² = c² ∇²uWaves / convection
▸ SOLUTION ROUTES
Analytical:  separation of variables → Fourier series
Numerical:   finite difference / finite element / finite volume
             (what every CFD & simulation package does)
🔬 The unsteady diffusion/heat equation ∂u/∂t = α ∂²u/∂x² recurs across the whole curriculum — transient conduction, drug release, and Fick's second law are all the same PDE.
➡️ Vector Calculus & the Transport Operators FIELDS ▶
The Language of Fields
grad, div & curl run the equations of change
Transport phenomena are written in vector calculus. Three operators — gradient, divergence, curl — encode how scalar and vector fields vary in space.
▸ THE THREE OPERATORS
OperatorActs on → givesMeaning
Gradient ∇φscalar → vectorDirection of steepest increase (drives flux)
Divergence ∇·vvector → scalarNet outflow per volume (source/sink)
Curl ∇×vvector → vectorLocal rotation (vorticity)
▸ WHERE THEY APPEAR
Fourier / Fick:  flux = −k∇T ,  −D∇C   (gradient drives transport)
Continuity:      ∂ρ/∂t + ∇·(ρv) = 0   (divergence = accumulation)
Laplacian ∇²:    steady diffusion/conduction (∇²φ = 0)
🔬 Every "equation of change" in Transport Phenomena is a balance written with these operators — recognizing them turns intimidating PDEs into familiar ideas.
🧫 Design of Experiments (DOE) EXPERIMENTAL ▶
Learn More with Fewer Runs
Vary factors together, not one at a time
Changing one variable at a time is slow and misses interactions. Statistical design of experiments plans efficient runs that reveal main effects and interactions.
▸ DESIGN TYPES
DesignUse
Full factorial (2ᵏ)All factor combinations — effects + interactions
Fractional factorialScreen many factors cheaply
Response surface (RSM)Find an optimum (curvature)
▸ WHY IT WINS
One-factor-at-a-time misses interactions & wastes runs.
A 2³ factorial = 8 runs gives 3 main effects + all interactions.
Randomize & replicate to separate signal from noise.
🎯 DOE is the backbone of Quality by Design (Pharma) and process optimization — the efficient path from lab to a validated design space.
📏 Error Analysis & Propagation of Uncertainty MEASUREMENT ▶
Every Number Has Error Bars
How input uncertainty flows to results
No measurement is exact. Propagation of uncertainty tells you how errors in inputs combine into the error of a calculated result — essential for honest engineering conclusions.
▸ PROPAGATION FORMULAS
Add/subtract:  σ_R² = σ_A² + σ_B²        (absolute errors add in quadrature)
Multiply/divide: (σ_R/R)² = (σ_A/A)² + (σ_B/B)²  (relative errors)
General: σ_R² = Σ (∂R/∂x_i)² σ_{x_i}²   (Taylor / delta method)
▸ ERROR TYPES
TypeCharacter
RandomScatter — reduced by averaging
SystematicBias — not reduced by averaging (calibrate it out)
🎯 The least-precise input usually dominates the result's error — improving it is where measurement effort pays off (identify it via the propagation terms).
📉 Interpolation & Curve Fitting NUMERICAL ▶
Reading Between the Data
Estimating values and fitting models
Property tables and experiments give discrete points. Interpolation estimates between them; curve fitting finds a model that best represents them.
▸ INTERPOLATION vs FITTING
MethodPasses through points?
Linear interpolationYes — between two points (steam tables!)
SplineYes — smooth piecewise polynomials
Least-squares fitNo — best trend through scatter
▸ LINEAR INTERPOLATION
y = y₁ + (x − x₁)·(y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁)

The steam-table tool in the Calculators domain uses exactly this.
⚠️ Don't extrapolate a fit far beyond its data — and beware high-order polynomials that oscillate wildly (use splines instead).
