💠 Molecular Diffusion — Fick's Laws FOUNDATION ▶
Species Move Down Gradients
The mass-transfer analog of Fourier's law
Separations exploit differences in how fast species move between phases. At the molecular scale that motion obeys Fick's laws.
▸ FICK'S LAWS
1st law (steady):   J_A = −D_AB (dC_A/dz)
2nd law (transient): ∂C_A/∂t = D_AB ∂²C_A/∂z²

D_AB = binary diffusivity  (gas ≫ liquid ≫ solid)
Flux driven by a concentration gradient.
▸ THE TRANSPORT ANALOGY
TransportFlux lawDiffusivity
Momentumτ = −μ dv/dyν = μ/ρ
Heatq = −k dT/dyα = k/ρc_p
MassJ = −D dC/dyD_AB
🔬 The three transport diffusivities have identical units (m²/s) — the basis of the Prandtl / Schmidt / Lewis number analogies.
🗼 Distillation & the McCabe–Thiele Method SEPARATION ▶
Stage-by-Stage Separation
Trading reflux for stages
The most important separation in the chemical industry. McCabe–Thiele graphically counts the equilibrium stages a column needs for a target purity.
▸ OPERATING LINES
Rectifying:  y = (R/(R+1)) x + x_D/(R+1)
Stripping:   y = (L̄/V̄) x − (W/V̄) x_W
q-line:      slope = q/(q−1)   (feed condition)

R = reflux ratio (L/D)
▸ LIMITING CASES
CaseMeaning
Total refluxMinimum stages (N_min, Fenske)
Minimum refluxInfinite stages (R_min, Underwood)
Actual designR ≈ 1.2–1.5 × R_min (Gilliland)
⚖️ More reflux ⇒ fewer stages but higher energy. The reflux/stage trade-off is the central economic decision in column design.
🧴 Absorption, Extraction & Membranes SURVEY ▶
Other Separation Routes
When distillation isn't the answer
Heat-sensitive, dilute, or close-boiling systems call for separations driven by solubility, partitioning, or selective permeation.
▸ SEPARATION MENU
OperationDriving propertyTypical use
Gas absorptionSolubility (Henry's law)Scrubbing CO₂, H₂S from gas
Liquid extractionPartition coefficientRecover antibiotics, aromatics
MembranesSelective permeabilityRO desalination, gas separation
AdsorptionSurface affinityDrying, PSA O₂/N₂, water polishing
▸ HENRY'S LAW
p_A = H · x_A      (dilute gas dissolving in liquid)
H = Henry's constant  (↑ H ⇒ less soluble)
🧬 Membrane separations (ultrafiltration, RO, chromatography) are central to downstream bioprocessing — see the Biochemical domain.
🎞️ Two-Film Theory & Overall Coefficients INTERPHASE ▶
Resistances in Series
Where does the mass-transfer resistance live?
At a gas-liquid interface, two stagnant films resist transfer. The two-film theory adds them like resistors in series, giving an overall coefficient — the mass-transfer analog of the U-value.
▸ OVERALL vs INDIVIDUAL COEFFICIENTS
1/K_y = 1/k_y + m/k_x        (gas-phase overall)
1/K_x = 1/(m·k_y) + 1/k_x    (liquid-phase overall)

m = slope of the equilibrium line (Henry's constant form).
▸ WHICH FILM CONTROLS?
ConditionControlling resistance
Very soluble gas (small m)Gas film controls
Sparingly soluble gas (large m)Liquid film controls
🎯 Design effort should target the controlling film — improving the other one barely moves the overall rate.
🏛️ Packed Columns — HTU & NTU CONTINUOUS CONTACT ▶
Sizing a Packed Absorber
Height = how hard × how many
Unlike tray columns, packed columns transfer mass continuously. Their height is the product of a transfer-unit height (HTU) and the number of transfer units (NTU).
▸ COLUMN HEIGHT
Z = HTU × NTU

HTU = G / (K_y a)      (mass-transfer efficiency of the packing)
NTU = ∫ dy / (y − y*)  (difficulty of the separation)

Dilute case:  NTU ≈ (y_in − y_out)/(driving force, log-mean)
▸ HTU vs HETP
ConceptApplies to
HTU / NTUContinuous-contact (packed) — rate based
HETP / stagesEquivalent tray height for packing
🌫️ Small HTU = efficient packing. Flooding sets the upper gas rate — design at ~70% of flooding velocity.
🗜️ Multicomponent Distillation — Fenske-Underwood-Gilliland SHORTCUT ▶
Beyond Binaries
Three correlations size a real column fast
Real columns separate many components. The FUG shortcut estimates minimum stages, minimum reflux and the actual stage count without a full stage-by-stage solve.
▸ THE THREE STEPS
MethodGives
FenskeMinimum stages N_min (total reflux)
UnderwoodMinimum reflux ratio R_min
GillilandActual N at a chosen R (correlation)
▸ FENSKE EQUATION
N_min = ln[ (x_LK/x_HK)_dist · (x_HK/x_LK)_bot ] / ln(α_LK,HK)

LK = light key, HK = heavy key, α = relative volatility.
🔑 Pick the light & heavy key components — the pair whose split you specify. Everything else distributes around them.
🧽 Adsorption — Isotherms & Breakthrough SORPTION ▶
Capturing on a Surface
Equilibrium isotherms and the moving front
Adsorption binds molecules onto a solid surface (activated carbon, zeolite, silica gel). An isotherm sets capacity; a breakthrough curve sets bed life.
▸ EQUILIBRIUM ISOTHERMS
Langmuir:   q = q_max·K·C / (1 + K·C)   (monolayer, saturates)
Freundlich: q = K·C^(1/n)               (empirical, heterogeneous)
▸ FIXED-BED OPERATION
ConceptMeaning
Mass-transfer zoneThe moving band where adsorption happens
BreakthroughOutlet conc. rises → bed nearly spent
RegenerationHeat (TSA) or pressure (PSA) swing to reuse
🌀 Pressure-swing adsorption (PSA) makes on-site N₂ and O₂ and purifies H₂ — cyclic beds adsorbing at high P and regenerating at low P.
🫙 Leaching & Solid-Liquid Extraction SEPARATION ▶
Dissolving Out the Valuable Part
A solvent pulls solute from a solid
Leaching contacts a solid with a solvent to dissolve a target — extracting metals from ore, oil from seeds, or caffeine from beans. It's mass transfer from within a particle.
▸ RATE-CONTROLLING STEPS
1. Solvent diffuses into the solid pores
2. Solute dissolves
3. Solute diffuses back out to the bulk

Fine grinding & agitation speed steps 1 & 3 (shorter diffusion path).
▸ CONTACTING SCHEMES
SchemeFeature
Counter-current stagesHigh yield, low solvent use
Percolation / heapSolvent trickles through a bed (ore heaps)
⛏️ Heap leaching recovers copper and gold from low-grade ore — then electrowinning (Electrochemical) finishes the metal.
🫧 Supercritical Fluid Extraction GREEN SEPARATION ▶
Neither Gas nor Liquid
Tunable solvents above the critical point
Above its critical point a fluid (often CO₂) has gas-like diffusivity and liquid-like density — a tunable, benign solvent for extraction, prized where solvent residue can't be tolerated.
▸ WHY SUPERCRITICAL CO₂
PropertyBenefit
Tunable solvencyChange density (P,T) → change what dissolves
Clean removalDepressurize → CO₂ leaves as gas, no residue
BenignNon-toxic, non-flammable, mild T (protects product)
▸ THE PROCESS
Compress CO₂ above Tc(31°C)/Pc(74 bar) → extract solute
→ drop pressure → solute precipitates, CO₂ recycled.
Uses: decaf coffee, hops, flavors, pharmaceuticals.
☕ Decaffeinating coffee with supercritical CO₂ leaves no solvent residue — a flagship green-separations success.
🔁 Ion Exchange SORPTION ▶
Swapping Ions on a Resin
Reversible ion capture for water & recovery
Ion-exchange resins trade harmless ions for target ions in solution — softening and deionizing water, recovering metals, and purifying products. Like adsorption, it's a cyclic fixed-bed operation.
▸ HOW IT WORKS
Cation resin (Na⁺ form) + Ca²⁺ → resin-Ca + 2Na⁺  (softening)
Deionization: cation (H⁺) + anion (OH⁻) resins → pure water
Regenerate with concentrated acid/base/brine when spent.
▸ USES
ApplicationPurpose
Water softeningRemove Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ hardness
DeionizationUltrapure water (boiler feed, electronics)
Recovery / purificationMetals, pharmaceuticals, chromatography
💧 Ion exchange shares the breakthrough-curve behavior of adsorption — the same mass- transfer-zone and regeneration concepts (see Adsorption).
🫖 Batch Distillation (Rayleigh) SEPARATION ▶
Distilling a Pot, Not a Column
Composition changes as you boil
Batch (differential) distillation boils a charge and collects vapor over time — flexible for small volumes and multiproduct plants, but the still composition drifts as the light component leaves.
▸ THE RAYLEIGH EQUATION
ln(L/L₀) = ∫ dx / (y − x)   (from initial to final still comp.)

L = moles left in the still; y in equilibrium with still x.
As boiling proceeds, the still (and product) get heavier.
▸ BATCH vs CONTINUOUS
BatchContinuous
Flexible, multiproductEfficient, steady composition
Composition driftsConstant product spec
🥃 Common in fine chemicals, pharma and spirits — one still makes many products, at the cost of a changing cut composition (collect fractions to manage it).
