🔷 Crystal Structure & Phase Diagrams FOUNDATION ▶
Structure → Properties
How atomic arrangement dictates behavior
The macroscopic properties of a material — strength, ductility, conductivity — are set by how its atoms pack and which phases are present at a given temperature and composition.
▸ COMMON METALLIC LATTICES
StructurePackingExamples
BCC68% (less ductile)Fe (α), Cr, W
FCC74% (ductile)Al, Cu, Ni, Fe (γ)
HCP74% (anisotropic)Zn, Mg, Ti
▸ THE LEVER RULE (two-phase region)
Fraction of phase β = (C₀ − C_α) / (C_β − C_α)

Read compositions off the phase-diagram tie line;
the fractions balance like a lever about C₀.
🔩 The Fe–C phase diagram underlies all steel metallurgy — austenite, ferrite, cementite and the eutectoid at 0.76% C.
🧵 Polymers & Plastics MACROMOLECULES ▶
Long-Chain Molecules
A major product of the chemical industry
Polymers are giant chains built by linking monomers. Chain architecture and molecular weight control everything from a flexible film to a rigid engineering resin.
▸ POLYMERIZATION & CLASSES
TypeBehavior on heatingExample
ThermoplasticSoftens & remelts (recyclable)PE, PP, PET, PVC
ThermosetCross-links permanentlyEpoxy, phenolic
ElastomerReversible large strainRubber, silicone
▸ KEY PROPERTY
Tg = glass-transition temperature
  Below Tg → glassy, brittle
  Above Tg → rubbery, flexible

Degree of polymerization sets molecular weight ⇒ strength & viscosity.
🌱 Bio-based and biodegradable polymers (PLA, PHA) tie polymer engineering back to the Biochemical domain and sustainable design.
🦠 Corrosion & Materials Selection DEGRADATION ▶
The Electrochemistry of Failure
Why plants rust — and how to stop them
Corrosion is an electrochemical process that costs industry billions annually. Material choice, coatings and cathodic protection defend against it.
▸ THE CORROSION CELL
Anode (oxidation):   Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻   (metal is lost)
Cathode (reduction): O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻

Requires: anode + cathode + electrolyte + circuit
▸ CONTROL STRATEGIES
MethodIdea
Material upgradeStainless, Ni alloys, titanium
Coatings / liningPaint, glass, polymer barrier
Cathodic protectionSacrificial anode (Zn, Mg)
InhibitorsAdditives that passivate the surface
⚠️ Localized attack — pitting, crevice and stress-corrosion cracking — is far more dangerous than uniform corrosion because it is hidden until failure.
🔨 Mechanical Properties & Failure STRENGTH ▶
Will It Hold?
The stress-strain curve tells the story
Pressure vessels, piping and supports must carry load without yielding or fracturing. The stress-strain curve defines the key design limits.
▸ STRESS-STRAIN LANDMARKS
Stress σ = F/A     Strain ε = ΔL/L     E = σ/ε (Young's modulus)

Elastic region → Yield strength (permanent deformation begins)
             → Ultimate tensile strength (max)
             → Fracture
▸ FAILURE MODES
ModeDriven by
YieldingStatic overload past yield strength
FatigueCyclic loading below yield (S-N curve)
CreepSlow strain at high T over time
Brittle fractureFlaws + low temperature (ductile-brittle transition)
🧊 Carbon steel loses toughness in the cold — the ductile-to-brittle transition caused the Liberty-ship and many vessel failures. Choose materials for the coldest service T.
🔥 Heat Treatment & Phase Transformations METALLURGY ▶
Tuning Properties by Thermal History
Same composition, very different steel
Steel's strength and toughness depend as much on how it was cooled as on its composition. Controlling phase transformations is the heart of metallurgy.
▸ COMMON TREATMENTS
TreatmentCoolingResult
QuenchingFast (water/oil)Martensite — hard, brittle
TemperingReheat quenched steelTrades hardness for toughness
AnnealingSlow (furnace)Soft, ductile, stress-relieved
NormalizingAir coolFine, uniform grain
▸ THE TTT DIAGRAM
Time-Temperature-Transformation curves map cooling rate → phase.
Fast past the "nose" → martensite; slow → pearlite/ferrite.
🔧 Welding is an uncontrolled local heat treatment — the heat-affected zone can harden and crack, which is why post-weld heat treatment (PWHT) is often required.
🧩 Composites & Materials Selection DESIGN CHOICE ▶
The Right Material for the Job
Combining phases; choosing by performance index
Composites blend a strong fiber with a binding matrix to beat either alone. Selecting any material trades off strength, weight, corrosion, temperature and cost.
▸ RULE OF MIXTURES (stiffness)
E_composite = V_f·E_fiber + V_m·E_matrix   (loaded along fibers)

Fibers carry load; the matrix transfers stress & protects them.
Examples: carbon-fiber/epoxy, fiberglass, reinforced concrete.
▸ SELECTION DRIVERS (Ashby thinking)
RequirementMaximize / minimize
Light & stiffSpecific modulus E/ρ
Light & strongSpecific strength σ_y/ρ
Corrosive serviceAlloy/lining vs. environment
Cost-limitedPerformance per dollar
📐 An Ashby chart plots one property against another (e.g. strength vs density) so a performance index picks the best material family at a glance.
🏺 Ceramics, Glasses & Refractories NON-METALS ▶
Hard, Brittle & Heat-Resistant
Where metals can't take the heat or the acid
Ceramics and glasses resist high temperature and corrosion but are brittle. Refractories line furnaces and reactors that would melt or corrode any metal.
▸ PROPERTIES & TRADE-OFFS
PropertyCeramics
StrengthsHigh T, hardness, chemical inertness, low conductivity
WeaknessBrittle — fail suddenly, low fracture toughness
GlassAmorphous; corrosion-resistant lining (glass-lined vessels)
▸ REFRACTORIES
Line furnaces, kilns, reactors, transfer lines.
Chosen for service temperature, thermal shock, and slag/acid resistance
(fireclay, alumina, silicon carbide, zirconia).
🧱 Brittle materials fail from flaws in tension — designed to keep them in compression, like the refractory lining of a fired heater or an FCC regenerator.
🔩 Welding & Fabrication CONSTRUCTION ▶
Joining Metal Reliably
A local heat treatment with big consequences
Vessels and piping are welded, and a weld is a small, uncontrolled casting-plus-heat- treatment. Its quality decides whether a pressure boundary holds.
▸ THE HEAT-AFFECTED ZONE (HAZ)
Weld metal (melted) | HAZ (altered microstructure) | base metal

The HAZ can harden & embrittle → cracking.
Post-weld heat treatment (PWHT) relieves stress & softens the HAZ.
▸ INTEGRITY & INSPECTION
ConcernHandling
Weld defectsNDT: radiography, ultrasonic, dye penetrant
SensitizationStainless welds → intergranular corrosion (use L grades)
CodesASME BPVC / B31.3 govern qualification
🦺 Welds are the usual failure point of pressure equipment — inspection and PWHT are mechanical-integrity essentials (see Process Safety, PSM).
🧵 Polymer Processing — Extrusion & Molding SHAPING ▶
From Pellets to Parts
Melt, shape, solidify
Making a polymer is only half the story — shaping it into film, fiber or a part is a heat-transfer and non-Newtonian-flow problem at the heart of the plastics industry.
▸ MAJOR PROCESSES
ProcessMakes
ExtrusionContinuous shapes: pipe, film, sheet, fiber
Injection moldingComplex discrete parts
Blow moldingHollow parts: bottles
ThermoformingSheet → trays, packaging
▸ THE PHYSICS
Melt is shear-thinning (non-Newtonian) — viscosity drops under shear.
Cooling rate sets crystallinity → part properties & shrinkage.
Residence time & temperature must avoid thermal degradation.
🍯 Polymer melts are strongly shear-thinning (see Non-Newtonian Fluids) — the reason they flow through a die under pressure yet hold shape once out.
🔎 Corrosion Testing & Monitoring INTEGRITY ▶
Catching Wall Loss Before It Fails
Measuring corrosion rate in service
Selecting a corrosion-resistant material is step one; verifying it stays intact in service is ongoing. Testing and monitoring quantify wall loss before it becomes a leak.
▸ METHODS
MethodMeasures
CouponsWeight loss over time (mpy / mm/yr)
UT thicknessWall thickness by ultrasound (inspection)
ER / LPR probesOnline corrosion-rate signal
RBIRisk-based inspection prioritizes what to check
▸ CORROSION ALLOWANCE
Design wall = required wall + corrosion allowance (e.g. 3 mm)
Monitor rate → predict remaining life → schedule replacement.
🦺 Mechanical integrity (a PSM element) depends on this — inspection data drives when to repair or replace before a loss of containment (see Process Safety).
