🧮 Laplace Transforms & Transfer Functions FOUNDATION ▶
Dynamics in the s-Domain
Turning ODEs into algebra
Process control lives in the frequency domain. The Laplace transform converts the differential equations of a process into algebraic transfer functions G(s) relating output to input.
▸ COMMON TRANSFORMS
f(t)F(s)
step, 11/s
e^(−at)1/(s+a)
df/dtsF(s) − f(0)
delay, f(t−θ)e^(−θs) F(s)
▸ FIRST-ORDER PROCESS
G(s) = Y(s)/U(s) = K / (τ s + 1)

K = process gain      τ = time constant
Step response reaches 63% of final value at t = τ.
⏱️ Add a dead time θ (e^(−θs)) and control gets harder — delay is the enemy of tight control.
🎚️ Feedback Control & PID Tuning CONTROLLER ▶
The Universal Controller
Proportional + Integral + Derivative
The PID controller drives the error (setpoint − measurement) to zero by combining three actions. It runs the overwhelming majority of industrial loops.
▸ THE PID LAW
u(t) = Kc [ e + (1/τ_I) ∫e dt + τ_D de/dt ]

e = setpoint − measured value
▸ WHAT EACH TERM DOES
TermEffectDownside
P (gain Kc)Fast responseLeaves offset
I (τ_I)Eliminates offsetCan add oscillation / windup
D (τ_D)Anticipates, dampsAmplifies noise
🔧 Ziegler–Nichols and IMC are the classic tuning recipes; too much gain pushes the loop past its stability limit into sustained oscillation.

〰️ Second-Order Dynamics & Response DYNAMICS ▶
Overshoot & Oscillation
The damping ratio shapes the response
Add inertia (or two first-order lags) and a process becomes second order — capable of overshoot and ringing. The damping ratio ζ decides how it settles.
▸ STANDARD SECOND-ORDER FORM
G(s) = K / (τ²s² + 2ζτs + 1)

τ = natural period      ζ = damping ratio
Overshoot & decay ratio depend only on ζ.
▸ RESPONSE REGIMES
ζBehavior
ζ < 1Underdamped — overshoots, oscillates
ζ = 1Critically damped — fastest, no overshoot
ζ > 1Overdamped — sluggish, no overshoot
🎛️ Tuning a loop too aggressively drops ζ toward 0 — the system rings and can go unstable. Most loops target ζ ≈ 0.4–0.7 for a fast, damped response.
📉 Frequency Response & Stability Margins STABILITY ▶
Will the Loop Stay Stable?
Bode plots and the margins that keep you safe
Feed a sinusoid through a process and it comes out shifted and scaled. Tracking gain and phase versus frequency (the Bode plot) reveals how close a loop is to instability.
▸ THE STABILITY CRITERION
Closed loop is unstable if, at the frequency where phase = −180°,
the open-loop gain |GH| ≥ 1.

Gain margin  = 1 / |GH| at phase crossover   (want > ~2, i.e. 6 dB)
Phase margin = 180° + phase at gain crossover (want > ~30–45°)
▸ WHAT ERODES STABILITY
FactorEffect
Higher controller gainFaster but less stable
Dead timeAdds phase lag — very destabilizing
More lags in seriesExtra −90° each — closes on −180°
🎛️ Margins are safety factors for the model: keep a healthy gain and phase margin so plant/model mismatch doesn't tip the loop into oscillation.
🪜 Cascade & Feedforward Control ADVANCED LOOPS ▶
Beyond a Single Feedback Loop
Reject disturbances before they hurt
Plain feedback only reacts after an error appears. Cascade and feedforward structures catch disturbances earlier for tighter control.
▸ THE STRUCTURES
SchemeIdeaBest for
CascadeInner fast loop + outer slow loopFast secondary disturbances (e.g. jacket temp)
FeedforwardMeasure the disturbance, act preemptivelyMeasurable, dominant disturbances
RatioHold one flow proportional to anotherBlending, combustion air/fuel
🔗 Feedforward is never perfect (model error), so it's almost always paired with a feedback trim loop that mops up the residual.
🚰 Control Valves & Final Control Elements HARDWARE ▶
Where Control Meets the Process
The valve that actually moves the flow
A controller's output is useless without a final element — usually a control valve. Sizing and characteristic selection make or break loop performance.
▸ VALVE SIZING (Cv)
Q = Cv · sqrt(ΔP / SG)      (liquid, turbulent)

Cv = flow coefficient (valve capacity)
Size for ~20–80% opening at normal flow (room to move both ways).
▸ INHERENT CHARACTERISTICS
CharacteristicBest when
LinearMost ΔP is across the valve
Equal-percentageValve ΔP varies with flow (common)
Quick-openingOn/off, relief
⚠️ Fail-safe action matters: choose air-to-open vs air-to-close so a signal or air loss drives the valve to the safe position (e.g. fuel valve fails closed).
🔢 Batch & Discrete Control (PLC / DCS) AUTOMATION ▶
Sequencing, Not Just Regulating
Recipes, steps and interlocks
Continuous control holds setpoints; batch and discrete control run sequences — charge, heat, react, discharge — enforced by logic and interlocks on PLCs and DCSs.
▸ CONTROL SYSTEMS
SystemStrength
DCSLarge continuous plants, many analog loops
PLCFast discrete logic, interlocks, machines
SCADASupervisory monitoring over wide areas
▸ BATCH STRUCTURE (ISA-88)
Recipe → unit procedures → operations → phases
Interlocks & permissives enforce safe sequencing
Safety Instrumented System (SIS) is separate & independent.
🦺 Keep the Safety Instrumented System independent of the control system — a shared failure shouldn't take out both control and protection (see Process Safety, SIL).
📡 Sensors & Transmitters MEASUREMENT ▶
You Can't Control What You Can't Measure
Turning a physical quantity into a signal
Every control loop starts with a measurement. Sensors convert temperature, pressure, flow and level into signals a controller can act on.
▸ COMMON MEASUREMENTS
VariableTypical sensor
TemperatureThermocouple, RTD
PressureDiaphragm / strain-gauge transmitter
FlowOrifice ΔP, Coriolis, magnetic, vortex
LevelDP cell, radar, ultrasonic
▸ SIGNAL BASICS
Analog standard: 4–20 mA current loop (noise-immune, live-zero)
Digital: fieldbus / HART overlay for diagnostics
Calibration & range set accuracy; drift needs periodic checks.
🔌 4–20 mA uses a "live zero" (4 mA = 0%) so a broken wire (0 mA) is distinguishable from a true zero reading — a built-in fault check.
🔧 Loop Tuning in Practice HANDS-ON ▶
Getting Loops to Behave
From twitchy to sluggish — finding the sweet spot
Textbook tuning rules are a starting point; real loops need judgment. Recognizing the symptoms of bad tuning gets you to a stable, responsive loop fast.
▸ SYMPTOMS → FIX
SymptomLikely cause / fix
OscillatingGain too high → reduce Kc
Slow to reach setpointGain too low / integral too slow
Persistent offsetAdd/strengthen integral action
Noisy/jumpy outputToo much derivative → reduce τ_D or filter
▸ METHODS
Ziegler-Nichols (ultimate gain) · Cohen-Coon (dead time)
IMC / lambda tuning (model-based, gentle & robust)
Start conservative; tighten until acceptable, keep margin.
🎛️ Most industrial loops are detuned on purpose for robustness — a slightly sluggish loop that never oscillates beats an aggressive one that rings when conditions change.
