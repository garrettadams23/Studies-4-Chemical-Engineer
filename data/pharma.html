💊 Drug Development & the API Pipeline FOUNDATION ▶
From Molecule to Medicine
Where chemical engineering meets the clinic
Making a drug is two problems: the active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) (reaction + separation) and the drug product (formulation into a dose). Both are unit-operations problems under intense regulation.
▸ DEVELOPMENT PHASES
StageFocus
Discovery / labRoute selection, mg–g scale
Process developmentScale-up, kg pilot, robustness
Clinical (I–III)GMP supply, growing batches
CommercialValidated, continuous or large batch
▸ WHAT AN API ACTUALLY LOOKS LIKE
🔬 CHIR-99021, a kinase inhibitor. Four rings, two chlorines and a flexible diamine linker — each ring is a separate synthesis step, and the whole molecule has to survive every one of them. That is why an API route runs 5–12 steps and why overall yield, not step yield, is the number that matters.
▸ API SYNTHESIS CHALLENGES
Multi-step synthesis · chiral (single-enantiomer) control
Crystallization for purity & polymorph control
Chromatography for tough separations
Low-volume, high-value → yield matters enormously
▸ SCREENING A CANDIDATE — LIPINSKI'S RULE OF FIVE

cLogP (lipophilicity)≤ 5

🧪 Pharma has high E-factors (lots of solvent waste per kg drug) — a growing driver for greener routes and continuous manufacturing.
✅ GMP, Validation & Quality by Design REGULATORY ▶
Proving the Process Is in Control
Quality is built in, not tested in
Pharmaceutical manufacturing runs under Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP). Modern practice designs quality into the process from the start (Quality by Design) rather than inspecting it at the end.
▸ VALIDATION (IQ / OQ / PQ)
StageConfirms
IQInstalled correctly (as specified)
OQOperates across its range
PQPerforms consistently in real production
▸ QUALITY BY DESIGN (ICH Q8–Q11)
Define the Quality Target Product Profile (QTPP)
→ identify Critical Quality Attributes (CQAs)
→ link to Critical Process Parameters (CPPs)
→ establish a Design Space (proven safe operating region)
→ monitor with PAT (Process Analytical Technology)
📋 The "design space" is a multivariate operating window — move within it freely without a new regulatory filing. It's essentially a validated process map.
🔁 Continuous Manufacturing & PAT MODERN PHARMA ▶
From Batch to Flow
Running pharma like a chemical plant
Pharma has historically run in batches, but continuous manufacturing — steady flow with in-line monitoring — improves consistency, shrinks footprint and enables real-time release.
▸ BATCH vs CONTINUOUS
AspectBatchContinuous
Scale-upBigger vesselsRun longer / more lines
QualityTest afterMonitored in real time (PAT)
FootprintLargeCompact
▸ PAT — PROCESS ANALYTICAL TECHNOLOGY
In-line sensors (NIR, Raman) measure quality attributes live
→ feedback control keeps the process in its design space
→ "real-time release" instead of end-of-line lab testing
🎛️ PAT turns QbD (see the GMP topic) into live control — the same feedback principles as the Process Control domain, applied to product quality.
🧴 Sterile & Aseptic Processing CONTAMINATION CONTROL ▶
Making Injectables Safe
Keeping microbes out of the product
Injectable drugs must be sterile. Where terminal sterilization would destroy the product, aseptic processing assembles it from sterile components in a controlled cleanroom.
▸ ROUTES TO STERILITY
ApproachHow
Terminal sterilizationSterilize in the final container (heat/radiation) — preferred
Aseptic processingSterilize components, fill in a cleanroom (heat-sensitive)
▸ CLEANROOM CLASSES
Grade A (ISO 5): critical fill zone — near-zero particles
Grades B/C/D: surrounding support areas, progressively less strict
HEPA-filtered laminar flow · gowning · environmental monitoring
🔬 Sterilization kinetics are first-order thermal death (see Biochemical) — the same Del factor governs autoclaving vials and media.

💊 Tablet & Solid Dosage Manufacturing DRUG PRODUCT ▶
From API Powder to Pill
Particle technology meets pharma
Most drugs are taken as tablets. Turning API powder into a uniform, reliable tablet is a particle-technology and unit-operations problem under GMP.
▸ THE TABLET PROCESS
API + excipients → blend → granulate (flow & uniformity)
→ dry → compress (tablet press) → coat → package

Critical quality attributes: content uniformity, hardness,
dissolution (how fast the drug releases).
▸ WHY EXCIPIENTS
ExcipientRole
Filler / diluentBulk up a tiny API dose
BinderHold the tablet together
DisintegrantBreak up in the body to release drug
LubricantPrevent sticking in the press
💊 Powder flow and granule quality (see Particle Technology) directly control tablet weight and dose uniformity — a patient-safety-critical link.
🧼 Cleanrooms & Contamination Control CORE ▶
Engineering Clean Air
Classifying and controlling particulate contamination
Sterile drug manufacturing happens in cleanrooms where airborne particle counts are held to strict limits by HEPA-filtered, pressure-cascaded air.
▸ CLASSIFICATION (ISO 14644)
GradeRoughlyUse
ISO 5 (Grade A)≤ 3,520 /m³ (≥0.5 µm)Aseptic fill zone
ISO 7 (Grade B/C)≤ 352,000 /m³Surrounding areas
ISO 8 (Grade D)≤ 3.52 M /m³Support / prep
▸ CONTROL STRATEGY
HEPA filtration + laminar (unidirectional) flow
Pressure cascade: cleaner rooms held at higher pressure
Gowning airlocks · surface disinfection · people = #1 particle source
🚪 Airflow direction is a design tool: positive pressure keeps dirt out of sterile areas; negative pressure keeps hazardous or live agents in.
🧫 Bioprocessing & Biologics APPLIED ▶
Making Drugs with Cells
Upstream fermentation & downstream purification
Modern biologics — antibodies, vaccines, insulin — are made by living cells in bioreactors, then purified through a chain of separation steps. It links Biochemical Eng. to GMP.
▸ THE TWO HALVES
Upstream:   cell culture / fermentation in a bioreactor
            (control T, pH, dissolved O₂, feed)
Downstream: harvest → centrifuge/filter → chromatography
            → viral clearance → formulation & fill
▸ WHY IT'S HARD
ChallengeReason
Shear sensitivityCells damaged by aggressive mixing
SterilityOne contaminant ruins a whole batch
Purity of complex moleculesChromatography-heavy, costly
🧬 Single-use (disposable) bioreactors have transformed the field — no clean-in-place, faster changeover, ideal for multi-product and continuous facilities.
