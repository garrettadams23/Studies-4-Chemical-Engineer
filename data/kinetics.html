📈 Rate Laws & the Arrhenius Equation FOUNDATION ▶
How Fast & Why
Concentration and temperature set the rate
Kinetics answers how fast a reaction proceeds — the dimension thermodynamics ignores. Rate laws are empirical; the Arrhenius form captures the steep temperature dependence.
▸ RATE LAW & ORDER
−r_A = k · C_A^a · C_B^b        order = a + b

Zero order:  C_A = C_A0 − kt
First order: ln(C_A/C_A0) = −kt      (t½ = ln2/k)
Second:      1/C_A − 1/C_A0 = kt
▸ ARRHENIUS TEMPERATURE LAW
k = A · exp(−E_a / R T)

ln k = ln A − (E_a/R)(1/T)   → straight line

A   = pre-exponential (frequency) factor
E_a = activation energy   (↑ E_a ⇒ more T-sensitive)
🌡️ Rule of thumb: many reaction rates roughly double for every +10 °C — a direct consequence of the exponential Arrhenius term.
🛢️ Ideal Reactors — Batch, CSTR & PFR DESIGN ▶
Three Design Equations
Mixing pattern decides the size
Every reactor design starts from a mole balance on the limiting reactant. Idealized mixing gives three canonical models.
▸ DESIGN EQUATIONS
ReactorMole balanceMixing
BatchN_A0 dX/dt = −r_A VPerfect, transient
CSTRV = F_A0 X / (−r_A)Perfectly mixed, uniform
PFRV = F_A0 ∫ dX/(−r_A)No axial mixing (plug)
▸ CSTR vs. PFR
For positive-order kinetics at equal conversion:
  V_PFR  <  V_CSTR     (PFR is smaller / more efficient)

CSTR operates at the LOW exit concentration everywhere,
so its average rate is lower ⇒ it needs more volume.
🔗 CSTRs in series approach PFR behavior as N → ∞ — a practical way to get plug-flow performance with stirred tanks.
✨ Selectivity, Yield & Catalysis MULTIPLE REACTIONS ▶
Getting the Right Product
Conversion isn't the whole story
When side reactions compete, maximizing the desired product matters more than raw conversion. Catalysts change the game by lowering E_a.
▸ KEY METRICS
Conversion X = (A reacted)/(A fed)
Selectivity  = (desired formed)/(undesired formed)
Yield        = (desired formed)/(A fed)
▸ CATALYSIS
TypeExample
HomogeneousAcid catalysis, organometallics (same phase)
HeterogeneousSolid catalyst, gas/liquid reactant (Haber, catalytic cracking)
EnzymaticBiological catalysts — see Enzyme Kinetics (Bio domain)

⚗️ A catalyst speeds both forward and reverse rates equally — it changes the path, never the equilibrium position.
⏳ Residence-Time Distribution (RTD) NON-IDEAL ▶
Real Reactors Aren't Ideal
A tracer test reveals the flow pattern
Real vessels channel, short-circuit and have dead zones. The residence-time distribution E(t), measured by a tracer test, quantifies how long fluid elements actually stay — bridging ideal models and reality.
▸ THE RTD FUNCTION
E(t) dt = fraction of exit stream with age t → t+dt
∫₀^∞ E(t) dt = 1        mean residence time  t̄ = ∫ t E(t) dt

Pulse (δ) tracer → E(t) directly
Step tracer      → F(t), and E = dF/dt
▸ IDEAL SIGNATURES
ReactorRTD signature
PFRSharp spike at t = τ (no spread)
CSTRExponential decay E = e^(−t/τ)/τ
Real vesselBetween the two; models: tanks-in-series, dispersion
🔬 RTD fixes the flow pattern but not conversion alone — for non-first-order kinetics you also need the micromixing (segregation) model.
🌡️ Non-Isothermal Reactors & Multiple Steady States ENERGY BALANCE ▶
Heat and Rate Are Coupled
Temperature feeds back on the reaction
Reactions release or absorb heat, and rate depends exponentially on temperature (Arrhenius). Coupling the mole and energy balances can produce multiple steady states and runaway.
▸ CSTR ENERGY BALANCE
Heat generated  G(T) = (−ΔH_rxn) · (−r_A) V   (S-shaped in T)
Heat removed    R(T) = ṁ c_p (T − T_0) + UA(T − T_c)  (linear)

Steady state where G(T) = R(T). Up to 3 intersections
→ low, unstable middle, and high (ignited) steady states.
▸ OPERATING CONCERNS
PhenomenonCause
Thermal runawayRemoval can't keep up with generation
Ignition/extinctionJump between steady states
Adiabatic ΔT(−ΔH)·C_A0 / (ρ c_p) — max temp rise
🚨 Exothermic reactors need enough cooling and a stable operating point — a core process-safety concern (calorimetry sizes the hazard).
🧽 Heterogeneous Catalysis — Thiele Modulus & Effectiveness DIFFUSION-REACTION ▶
Does the Pore Interior Get Used?
Reaction vs diffusion inside a catalyst pellet
Inside a porous catalyst, reactant must diffuse in while it reacts. The Thiele modulus compares the two rates; the effectiveness factor says how much of the pellet actually works.
▸ THIELE MODULUS & EFFECTIVENESS
φ = L · sqrt(k / D_eff)      (reaction rate / diffusion rate)

η = (actual rate) / (rate with no diffusion limit)

φ ≪ 1  → η ≈ 1  (reaction-limited, whole pellet used)
φ ≫ 1  → η ≈ 1/φ (diffusion-limited, only the shell works)
🔬 Strong pore-diffusion limitation halves the apparent activation energy and makes rate scale with sqrt(k) — a classic diagnostic of internal diffusion control.
📊 Analyzing Rate Data — Integral & Differential Methods LAB → RATE LAW ▶
From Measurements to Kinetics
Extracting order and rate constant from data
Rate laws are found, not assumed. Two classic methods turn concentration-vs-time (or rate) data into an order and a rate constant.
▸ THE TWO METHODS
MethodIdea
IntegralGuess order → plot the linearized form → check for a straight line
DifferentialGet −r_A = −dC_A/dt (slopes), then ln(−r_A) vs ln C_A → order = slope
▸ LINEARIZED INTEGRAL PLOTS
Zero order:  C_A      vs t   → straight
First order: ln C_A   vs t   → straight (slope −k)
Second:      1/C_A    vs t   → straight (slope k)
📈 The order whose plot is straightest wins. The method of initial rates (differential at t=0) avoids interference from products.
🪫 Catalyst Deactivation & Regeneration CATALYST LIFE ▶
Catalysts Wear Out
Activity fades — design must plan for it
Real catalysts lose activity over time. Understanding the mechanism sets the reactor type, operating strategy and regeneration schedule.
▸ DEACTIVATION MECHANISMS
MechanismCauseReversible?
Coking / foulingCarbon deposits block sitesYes (burn off)
PoisoningImpurity (S, Pb) binds sitesOften no
SinteringThermal — active area shrinksNo
▸ COPING STRATEGIES
Raise T over a run to hold conversion (until end-of-run)
Continuous regeneration (FCC burns coke every cycle)
Guard beds remove poisons upstream
🔥 The FCC's genius is turning fast coking into an asset — burning the coke off the circulating catalyst supplies the heat for cracking (see Petroleum).
🫧 Multiphase & Gas-Liquid Reactors REACTOR TYPES ▶
Reactions Across Phases
When mass transfer and reaction compete
Many industrial reactions span phases — gas into liquid, over a solid catalyst. Here mass transfer between phases can control the overall rate as much as the kinetics.
▸ COMMON MULTIPHASE REACTORS
ReactorPhasesExample
Bubble columnGas–liquidOxidations, fermentation
Trickle bedGas–liquid–solidHydrotreating
Slurry reactorGas–liquid–fine solidHydrogenation, F-T
▸ WHO CONTROLS?
Overall rate limited by the slowest step:
  gas-film · liquid-film · pore diffusion · surface reaction.
Hatta number compares reaction rate to diffusion in the film
(fast reaction → transfer-limited, enhanced absorption).
🔗 It's the two-film theory (Mass Transfer) plus catalysis (Thiele modulus) in one unit — design targets whichever resistance dominates.
🧵 Polymerization Kinetics MACROMOLECULES ▶
Building Chains, Kinetically
Chain vs step growth set the product
Polymer properties depend on chain length and its distribution — which are set by the polymerization kinetics and reactor conditions.
▸ TWO MECHANISMS
TypeBehaviorExample
Chain growthInitiate → propagate fast → terminatePolyethylene, PVC (radical)
Step growthAny two ends react; MW builds slowlyNylon, PET (condensation)
▸ KEY IDEAS
Degree of polymerization sets molecular weight → strength/viscosity.
Chain growth: high MW early, even at low conversion.
Step growth: high MW only at very high conversion (need >99%).
Exothermic → heat removal is critical (runaway risk).
🌡️ Polymerizations are strongly exothermic and get viscous (poor mixing/cooling) — a reaction-engineering and heat-transfer challenge (gel effect / runaway).
🔑 Enzyme & Biocatalytic Reactor Design BIOCATALYSIS ▶
Reactors Run by Enzymes
Michaelis–Menten meets reactor design
Enzymes are exquisite catalysts. Designing reactors around them combines Michaelis–Menten kinetics with reactor mole balances — and the practicality of reusing the enzyme.
▸ IMMOBILIZED-ENZYME REACTORS
ConfigFeature
Packed-bed (PFR-like)Enzyme on a support; continuous, reusable
CSTR / membraneRetain enzyme, remove product
Batch (free enzyme)Simple, but enzyme is lost
▸ DESIGN NOTE
Combine −r = V_max[S]/(K_m+[S]) with the reactor balance.
Immobilization adds pore-diffusion limits (Thiele modulus again!)
and can shift the apparent K_m.
🔗 The same diffusion-reaction physics (Thiele modulus, effectiveness) from heterogeneous catalysis reappears for immobilized enzymes — one idea, two worlds.
🔥 Combustion & Chain Reactions FREE RADICALS ▶
Reactions That Feed Themselves
Radicals, branching, and explosion
Combustion and many gas-phase reactions proceed by chain reactions — free radicals that propagate and can branch, accelerating the rate explosively.
▸ CHAIN-REACTION STEPS
Initiation:  form radicals (e.g. bond breaks by heat)
Propagation: radical + molecule → product + new radical
Branching:   one radical → two (accelerates → explosion)
Termination: radicals recombine (chain ends)
▸ EXPLOSION LIMITS
ControlEffect
Chain branchingRadicals multiply → thermal + chain explosion
Wall terminationRadicals quench on surfaces (small vessels safer)
🚨 Branching chains explain why some mixtures ignite explosively within specific pressure/ temperature "explosion limits" — key to combustion and process safety.
