📈 Rate Laws & the Arrhenius Equation FOUNDATION ▶
How Fast & Why
Concentration and temperature set the rate
Kinetics answers how fast a reaction proceeds — the dimension thermodynamics ignores. Rate laws are empirical; the Arrhenius form captures the steep temperature dependence.
▸ RATE LAW & ORDER
−r_A = k · C_A^a · C_B^b        order = a + b

Zero order:  C_A = C_A0 − kt
First order: ln(C_A/C_A0) = −kt      (t½ = ln2/k)
Second:      1/C_A − 1/C_A0 = kt
▸ ARRHENIUS TEMPERATURE LAW
k = A · exp(−E_a / R T)

ln k = ln A − (E_a/R)(1/T)   → straight line

A   = pre-exponential (frequency) factor
E_a = activation energy   (↑ E_a ⇒ more T-sensitive)
🌡️ Rule of thumb: many reaction rates roughly double for every +10 °C — a direct consequence of the exponential Arrhenius term.
🛢️ Ideal Reactors — Batch, CSTR & PFR DESIGN ▶
Three Design Equations
Mixing pattern decides the size
Every reactor design starts from a mole balance on the limiting reactant. Idealized mixing gives three canonical models.
▸ DESIGN EQUATIONS
ReactorMole balanceMixing
BatchN_A0 dX/dt = −r_A VPerfect, transient
CSTRV = F_A0 X / (−r_A)Perfectly mixed, uniform
PFRV = F_A0 ∫ dX/(−r_A)No axial mixing (plug)
▸ CSTR vs. PFR
For positive-order kinetics at equal conversion:
  V_PFR  <  V_CSTR     (PFR is smaller / more efficient)

CSTR operates at the LOW exit concentration everywhere,
so its average rate is lower ⇒ it needs more volume.
🔗 CSTRs in series approach PFR behavior as N → ∞ — a practical way to get plug-flow performance with stirred tanks.
✨ Selectivity, Yield & Catalysis MULTIPLE REACTIONS ▶
Getting the Right Product
Conversion isn't the whole story
When side reactions compete, maximizing the desired product matters more than raw conversion. Catalysts change the game by lowering E_a.
▸ KEY METRICS
Conversion X = (A reacted)/(A fed)
Selectivity  = (desired formed)/(undesired formed)
Yield        = (desired formed)/(A fed)
▸ CATALYSIS
TypeExample
HomogeneousAcid catalysis, organometallics (same phase)
HeterogeneousSolid catalyst, gas/liquid reactant (Haber, catalytic cracking)
EnzymaticBiological catalysts — see Enzyme Kinetics (Bio domain)
⚗️ A catalyst speeds both forward and reverse rates equally — it changes the path, never the equilibrium position.
