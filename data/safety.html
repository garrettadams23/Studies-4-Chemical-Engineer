🔍 HAZOP, LOPA & Layers of Protection FOUNDATION ▶
Systematic Risk Assessment
Finding what can go wrong before it does
Process safety is a discipline of defense in depth. Structured methods systematically hunt for deviations and verify that enough independent safeguards stand between a cause and a catastrophe.
▸ HAZOP GUIDE WORDS
Guide wordDeviation
NO / NONENo flow, no reaction
MOREHigh flow, pressure, temperature
LESSLow flow, level, temperature
REVERSE / AS WELL ASBackflow, contamination
▸ LAYERS OF PROTECTION (LOPA)
1. Process design (inherent safety)
2. Basic process control (BPCS)
3. Alarms + operator action
4. Safety Instrumented System (SIS / SIL-rated)
5. Physical relief (PSV, rupture disk)
6. Containment (dike, blast wall)
7. Emergency response

Each independent layer reduces risk multiplicatively.
🛡️ The best safeguard is inherent safety — reduce inventory, substitute benign chemicals, moderate conditions — so hazards are designed out, not guarded against.

🌬️ Toxic Release & Dispersion Modeling CONSEQUENCE ▶
How Far Does It Reach?
Source term, then atmospheric dispersion
Consequence analysis asks how a release spreads and who it affects. It splits into a source term (release rate) and a dispersion model (downwind concentration).
▸ THE TWO STEPS
Source term:  liquid/gas discharge rate through the hole
              (Bernoulli / choked-flow equations)

Dispersion:  Gaussian plume  C(x,y,z) ∝ Q / (u σ_y σ_z)
             σ_y, σ_z grow downwind (Pasquill stability class)
▸ EXPOSURE LIMITS
LimitMeaning
TLV / PELSafe for an 8-h working day
ERPG-2Max for 1 h without serious effects
IDLHImmediately dangerous to life & health
🌫️ Dense gases (chlorine, ammonia) slump and hug the ground rather than dispersing upward — they need heavy-gas models, not the simple Gaussian plume.
🌳 Fault Tree & Event Tree Analysis QUANTITATIVE ▶
Putting Numbers on Risk
Backward from failure, forward from an event
Quantitative risk analysis links equipment failure rates to outcome frequencies. Fault trees work backward from a top event; event trees work forward from an initiating event.
▸ FAULT-TREE LOGIC
AND gate:  P = P₁ × P₂        (both must fail — redundancy)
OR gate:   P = P₁ + P₂ − P₁P₂ (either failure suffices)

Top-event frequency built up from component failure rates.
▸ THE TWO METHODS
ToolDirectionAnswers
Fault treeBackward (deductive)How can this failure happen?
Event treeForward (inductive)What outcomes follow this event?
🔗 Redundancy (AND logic) multiplies small probabilities — two independent safeguards at 0.01 each give a 0.0001 combined failure, the mathematics behind layers of protection.
🌡️ Reactive Chemicals & Runaway Reactions THERMAL HAZARD ▶
When Chemistry Gets Away From You
Self-accelerating heat release
A runaway reaction occurs when heat generation outpaces removal: temperature rises, which speeds the reaction (Arrhenius), which generates more heat — a vicious loop. Calorimetry quantifies the danger.
▸ THE RUNAWAY LOOP
↑T → ↑ rate (Arrhenius) → ↑ heat release → ↑T …

Cooling (linear in T) eventually can't match generation
(exponential in T) → thermal runaway, pressure rise, possible explosion.
▸ CALORIMETRY & DEFENSES
Tool / defenseRole
DSC / ARCMeasure onset T & heat of reaction
Adiabatic ΔT_maxWorst-case temperature rise
DefensesDumping, quench, emergency cooling, relief, inhibitor
🚨 Ties to Reaction Engineering (multiple steady states): exothermic reactors must have enough cooling AND a stable operating point — Seveso and T2 Labs were runaways.
