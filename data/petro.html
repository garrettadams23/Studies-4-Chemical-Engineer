🛢️ Crude Oil & Atmospheric Distillation FOUNDATION ▶
The Refinery's Front Door
One giant column splits crude into cuts
A refinery begins by separating crude oil into boiling-range cuts in a large distillation column — the biggest single application of the distillation you learned in Mass Transfer.
▸ THE DISTILLATION CUTS (light → heavy)
CutApprox. boiling rangeUse
Gases / LPG< 30 °CFuel gas, propane
Naphtha / gasoline30–200 °CGasoline, reformer feed
Kerosene / jet200–275 °CJet fuel
Diesel / gas oil275–350 °CDiesel, heating oil
Residue> 350 °CVacuum tower feed, asphalt
▸ ATMOSPHERIC + VACUUM
Crude → desalter → fired heater → atmospheric column
Residue → vacuum column (low P avoids thermal cracking of
heavy molecules at the required temperature)
🌡️ Heavy fractions are distilled under vacuum because heating them to their atmospheric boiling point would crack (decompose) them first.
⚗️ Conversion — Cracking & Hydroprocessing UPGRADING ▶
Turning Heavy into Light
Breaking big molecules into valuable fuels
Distillation only separates what's already there. Conversion units chemically crack heavy, low-value molecules into gasoline and diesel — reaction engineering and catalysis at massive scale.
▸ THE MAJOR CONVERSION UNITS
UnitDoesKey feature
FCCCatalytic cracking of gas oilFluidized catalyst, continuous regen
HydrocrackingCracking under H₂High-quality distillate, flexible
HydrotreatingRemoves S, N (HDS/HDN)Meets clean-fuel specs
CokingCracks residue to the limitMakes petroleum coke
▸ THE FCC RISER
Hot regenerated catalyst + gas oil → riser (seconds of contact)
→ cracked vapors to fractionator
→ spent (coked) catalyst → regenerator (burn off coke) → recycle

Coke burning supplies the heat for the endothermic cracking.
🔥 The FCC is a coupled reactor-regenerator with continuous catalyst circulation — a textbook fluidized-bed and heat-integration problem (see Unit Ops & Reaction Eng).
🌳 The Petrochemical Tree PRODUCTS ▶
From Oil to Everything
A handful of building blocks make the material world
Beyond fuels, refineries and crackers feed the petrochemical tree — a few key intermediates that branch into plastics, fibers, solvents and rubber.
▸ THE PRIMARY BUILDING BLOCKS
Building blockFromLeads to

▸ STEAM CRACKING
Naphtha/ethane + steam → 800+ °C, very short residence time
→ ethylene, propylene, butadiene, aromatics
Quench fast to lock in the product slate (kinetics, not equilibrium).
🧬 Ethylene is the single largest-volume organic chemical made — its polymers link back to the Materials domain, and its production is a kinetics/selectivity masterclass.
⛽ Catalytic Reforming & Octane UPGRADING ▶
Making Gasoline Burn Better
Turning low-octane naphtha into aromatics
Catalytic reforming rearranges straight-chain naphtha into branched and aromatic molecules — raising octane and producing the BTX aromatics that feed petrochemicals, plus hydrogen as a by-product.
▸ KEY REACTIONS
Dehydrogenation:  naphthenes → aromatics + H₂  (big octane gain)
Isomerization:    n-paraffins → branched (higher octane)
Dehydrocyclization: paraffins → aromatics + H₂

Pt/Re on alumina catalyst; H₂ is a valuable co-product.
▸ OCTANE NUMBER
Molecule typeOctane tendency
n-ParaffinsLow
Iso-paraffinsHigher
AromaticsHighest
⚗️ The reformer is a refinery's main hydrogen source — that H₂ then feeds the hydrotreaters and hydrocrackers (see the Conversion topic).
🔥 Natural Gas Processing & Sweetening GAS PLANT ▶
Cleaning Raw Gas to Spec
Removing the sour and the wet
Raw natural gas carries acid gases (H₂S, CO₂), water and heavy hydrocarbons. Processing removes them so the gas is safe, dry and pipeline-ready.
▸ THE PROCESSING TRAIN
1. Sweetening   — amine absorption removes H₂S & CO₂ (sour → sweet)
2. Dehydration  — glycol (TEG) absorption removes water
3. NGL recovery — recover ethane/propane/butane (cryo or absorption)
4. Sulfur       — Claus process turns H₂S into elemental sulfur
▸ WHY EACH STEP
ContaminantProblem
H₂SToxic, corrosive (sour)
CO₂Corrosive, lowers heating value
WaterHydrates & corrosion in pipelines
🧪 Amine sweetening is the same absorption/stripping as CO₂ capture (Sustainability) — one of the most widely deployed separation processes on earth.
💵 Refinery Economics & Configuration BUSINESS ▶
Buying Cheap, Selling Dear
Margins made by upgrading the barrel
A refinery's profit is the crack spread — the gap between product value and crude cost. Its configuration (how much conversion) sets how much value it can add.
▸ REFINERY COMPLEXITY
ConfigurationCapability
ToppingDistillation only — low upgrade
CrackingAdds FCC — more gasoline from heavy cuts
Coking / full conversionSqueeze value from the bottom of the barrel
▸ THE CRACK SPREAD
Crack spread ≈ (Σ product values) − (crude cost) − (operating cost)

More conversion → capture more value from cheap heavy crude,
at higher capital cost. It's an economics-vs-capex trade-off.
📈 A complex refinery buys cheap heavy/sour crude and upgrades it — the reason conversion units (FCC, coker, hydrocracker) earn their large capital cost.
