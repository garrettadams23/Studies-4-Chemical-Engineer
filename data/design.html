▸ HEAT INTEGRATION — PINCH ANALYSIS
Match hot & cold streams to recover heat internally.
The "pinch" sets the minimum utility (steam/cooling) needed.

Rule:  don't transfer heat across the pinch,
       don't cool above it, don't heat below it.
♻️ Pinch analysis can cut a plant's energy bill dramatically — the flagship tool of process integration and sustainable design.
💰 Engineering Economics & Profitability DECISION ▶
Is the Project Worth Building?
Time value of money decides
A technically brilliant process still has to make money. Capital and operating costs are weighed against revenue using discounted-cash-flow metrics.
▸ PROFITABILITY METRICS
Future value:  F = P(1 + i)ⁿ
Present value: P = F / (1 + i)ⁿ

NPV = Σ (cash flow_t) / (1 + i)^t  − capital
  NPV > 0  → accept
IRR = discount rate where NPV = 0
Payback = years to recover the capital outlay
▸ COST ESTIMATING SHORTCUTS
RuleRelation
Six-tenths ruleCost₂ = Cost₁ (S₂/S₁)^0.6
Lang factorFixed capital ≈ factor × equipment cost
CEPCIEscalate old costs to today's dollars
📊 The six-tenths rule captures economy of scale: doubling capacity rarely doubles cost — bigger is usually cheaper per unit.
🖥️ Process Simulation & Convergence TOOLS ▶
The Modern Design Workhorse
Flowsheet simulators do the heavy algebra
Tools like Aspen Plus, HYSYS and DWSIM solve thousands of coupled balance and equilibrium equations. The engineer's job is model selection, specification, and sanity-checking — not hand algebra.
▸ WHAT DRIVES ACCURACY
ChoiceWhy it matters
Thermo packagePeng-Robinson (hydrocarbons) vs NRTL (polar) — get this wrong and all results are wrong
Property dataBinary interaction parameters, missing components
Recycle convergenceTear streams, Wegstein/Newton iteration
▸ SEQUENTIAL-MODULAR SOLVE
Solve units in flow order; for recycles, "tear" a stream,
guess it, iterate until the guess matches the computed value.
⚠️ Garbage in, garbage out: a converged simulation is not a correct one. Always reality-check against hand balances and physical intuition.
🌿 Optimization & Green Design BEST DESIGN ▶
Best, Not Just Feasible
Minimize cost and environmental footprint
Design isn't done when it works — it's done when it's optimal. Formal optimization finds the best trade-off, and green-design metrics fold sustainability into the objective.
▸ OPTIMIZATION ANATOMY
minimize   f(x)          ← objective (cost, energy, emissions)
subject to g(x) ≤ 0      ← constraints (specs, safety, physics)

Linear → LP (simplex)   Nonlinear → NLP (SQP, gradient)
Mixed integer → MILP/MINLP (equipment on/off decisions)
▸ GREEN-DESIGN METRICS
MetricMeasures

♻️ The greenest, cheapest waste is the one never made — process intensification and high selectivity beat end-of-pipe treatment every time.
🔧 Utilities, Steam Levels & Cooling Water SITE SYSTEMS ▶
The Plant's Support Systems
Heat and cooling delivered as utilities
Processes rarely make their own heating and cooling — a central utility system supplies steam at several pressure levels, cooling water, refrigeration, power and inert gas.
▸ STEAM PRESSURE LEVELS
LevelTypical use
HP steamDrives turbines; highest-temperature heating
MP steamReboilers, general process heating
LP steamLow-temperature heating, stripping, tracing
▸ COOLING & SELECTION
Cooling water   ~30 °C supply (cooling-tower limited)
Chilled water   ~5–10 °C (refrigeration, energy-costly)
Air cooling     when water is scarce

Pick the cheapest utility that meets the ΔT the duty needs.
💰 Utility choice is an economics decision — refrigeration is far pricier than cooling water, so designs avoid low temperatures unless the separation truly needs them.
🗺️ Plant Layout & Safety in Design DETAILED DESIGN ▶
Where Things Go, and How Far Apart
Layout is a safety decision, not just a map
How equipment is arranged affects safety, operability, maintenance and cost. Spacing and siting bake hazard control into the plant before it's built.
▸ LAYOUT DRIVERS
DriverImplication
Hazard spacingSeparate ignition sources from flammables; fire breaks
Gravity & flowElevation for drainage, NPSH, columns
Maintenance accessCrane/laydown space, tube-pull room
Prevailing windSite control rooms & flares upwind/away
▸ INHERENTLY SAFER LAYOUT
Minimize inventory near people · locate high-hazard units at the edge
Provide escape routes & blast-resistant control rooms
Slope & dike to contain spills (see Process Safety)
🦺 Layout is the cheapest safety layer — decisions made on the plot plan are nearly free early and enormously expensive to change after construction.
🎛️ Process Intensification NEXT-GEN ▶
Smaller, Cleaner, Cheaper
Doing more in far less equipment
Process intensification radically shrinks equipment and energy by combining functions and enhancing transport — the frontier of sustainable design.
▸ INTENSIFICATION STRATEGIES
ApproachExample
Combine functionsReactive distillation (react + separate in one column)
Enhance transportMicroreactors, structured packing, spinning-disc
Alternative energyMicrowave, ultrasound, plasma
Continuous > batchFlow chemistry replacing batch reactors
▸ WHY IT MATTERS
Smaller inventory → inherently safer (less to release)
Less capital & energy → cheaper and greener
Better control of fast/exothermic reactions (microreactors)
🦺 Intensification is also inherent safety: a microreactor holds grams, not tonnes, so a runaway can't become a catastrophe (see Process Safety).
