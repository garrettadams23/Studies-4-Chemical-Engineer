🏭 The Unit-Operations Toolkit SURVEY ▶
Building Blocks of a Plant
Any process is a sequence of unit operations
The unit-operations concept (Arthur D. Little, 1915) recognizes that every process is assembled from a finite set of standard physical operations — the same distillation column serves oil refining and brewing alike.
▸ CLASSIFICATION
CategoryOperations
Fluid handlingPumps, compressors, fans, piping

Solids / mixingCrushing, crystallization, agitation, fluidization
🔧 Learn the operation once and you can apply it across every industry — the power of the unit-operations abstraction.

🪨 Size Reduction & Comminution SOLIDS ▶
Breaking Solids Down
Energy laws for crushing and grinding
Crushing and grinding are energy-hungry — much of the input becomes heat and noise. Three empirical laws relate energy to the change in particle size.
▸ THE THREE COMMINUTION LAWS
LawEnergy ∝Best for
Kickln(D₁/D₂)Coarse crushing
Rittingernew surface (1/D₂ − 1/D₁)Fine grinding
Bond(1/√D₂ − 1/√D₁)Intermediate (work index)
▸ BOND WORK INDEX
E = 10 W_i (1/√D₂ − 1/√D₁)     D in µm, E in kWh/ton

W_i = Bond work index (material property, from grindability tests)
⚙️ Grinding efficiency is notoriously low (~1%). Equipment: jaw/gyratory crushers (coarse) → ball/rod mills (fine) → jet mills (ultrafine).
🌀 Mixing & Agitation SCALE-UP ▶
Stirring It All Together
Power draw and the notorious scale-up problem
Agitation blends, suspends solids, disperses gas and transfers heat. Its power draw is correlated through the dimensionless power number.
▸ POWER CORRELATION
Power number  N_p = P / (ρ N³ D⁵)
Impeller Reynolds  Re = ρ N D² / μ

Turbulent (Re > 10⁴): N_p ≈ constant → P ∝ ρ N³ D⁵
Laminar (Re < 10):    N_p ∝ 1/Re     → P ∝ μ N² D³
▸ IMPELLER CHOICE
TypeBest for
Rushton turbineGas dispersion, high shear
Pitched-blade / hydrofoilBlending, solids suspension (axial flow)
Anchor / helicalHigh-viscosity, laminar
📐 You can't hold everything constant on scale-up — equal power/volume, equal tip speed, and equal mixing time are competing criteria. Pick the one that matters for your duty.
💨 Evaporation & Multiple-Effect Evaporators CONCENTRATION ▶
Boiling Off Solvent
Reusing steam across stages for efficiency
Evaporation concentrates a solution by boiling off solvent (usually water). Multiple-effect arrangements reuse the vapor from one effect as the heating steam for the next — a heat-integration classic.
▸ THE STEAM ECONOMY IDEA
Single effect: ~1 kg steam evaporates ~1 kg water
N effects in series: ~1 kg steam evaporates ~N kg water

Each effect runs at lower P (and lower T) so vapor from
effect i can boil the liquid in effect i+1.
▸ KEY EFFECTS
FactorImpact
Boiling-point riseDissolved solids raise the boiling T (less ΔT available)
Feed arrangementForward / backward / mixed feed trades economy vs viscosity
Vapor recompressionMVR/TVR boosts economy further
♻️ Multiple-effect evaporation is the same reuse-the-energy principle as pinch analysis — each effect's condensing vapor is the next one's heat source.
💫 Centrifugation & Sedimentation SEPARATION ▶
Separating by Density
Let gravity — or many g's — do the work
Solids or immiscible liquids separate by density difference. Sedimentation uses gravity; centrifugation multiplies it thousands of times for fine or slow-settling particles.
▸ TERMINAL (STOKES) SETTLING
v_t = d²(ρ_p − ρ_f) g / (18 μ)     (Stokes, small Re)

Centrifuge: replace g with ω²r → separation factor = ω²r/g
(often thousands — settles what gravity never could).
▸ EQUIPMENT
DeviceUse
Thickener / clarifierGravity settling, large flows
Disc-stack centrifugeFine solids, liquid-liquid
DecanterHigh-solids slurries
🧫 Centrifugation is the first step of most bioseparations — spinning cells out of broth (see Downstream Processing in Biochemical).
🏛️ Column Internals — Trays & Packing EQUIPMENT ▶
Where the Contacting Happens
Trays vs packing — the column's working guts
Distillation and absorption columns need intimate vapor-liquid contact. Two families of internals provide it — discrete trays or continuous packing — each with a niche.
▸ TRAYS vs PACKING
InternalBest for
Sieve / valve traysLarge columns, fouling service, wide turndown
Random packingLow pressure drop, corrosive service
Structured packingHigh efficiency, vacuum, low ΔP (HETP small)
▸ HYDRAULIC LIMITS
Too much vapor → flooding (liquid carried up)
Too little → weeping / dumping (liquid falls through)
Design between these at ~70–85% of flood.
🗼 Structured packing gives many stages in a short height at low pressure drop — ideal for vacuum distillation of heat-sensitive materials.

🫧 Froth Flotation SURFACE SEPARATION ▶
Separating by Wettability
Bubbles carry the right particles to the top
Flotation separates fine particles by differences in surface chemistry: air bubbles attach to water-repelling (hydrophobic) particles and float them off as a froth — the backbone of mineral processing.
▸ HOW IT WORKS
Collectors make target particles hydrophobic
→ air bubbled through the slurry attaches to them
→ they rise into a froth layer & are skimmed off
→ hydrophilic (wetted) particles stay in the liquid.
▸ THE REAGENTS
ReagentRole
CollectorSelectively makes a mineral hydrophobic
FrotherStabilizes the bubbles/froth
ModifierAdjusts pH & selectivity
⛏️ Flotation concentrates most of the world's copper, and its surface-chemistry principles extend to de-inking paper and even wastewater treatment.
