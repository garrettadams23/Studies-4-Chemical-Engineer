⚖️ Chemical Equilibrium & Le Chatelier EQUILIBRIA ▶
How Far Does a Reaction Go?
K sets the destination; Le Chatelier predicts the shift
Reactions reach a dynamic equilibrium where forward and reverse rates match. The equilibrium constant K fixes the position; Le Chatelier's principle predicts how disturbances shift it.
▸ EQUILIBRIUM CONSTANT
For  aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD :
  Kc = [C]^c [D]^d / ([A]^a [B]^b)

ΔG° = −RT ln K
van't Hoff:  d(ln K)/dT = ΔH° / RT²   (T-dependence)
▸ LE CHATELIER — STRESS → SHIFT
DisturbanceEquilibrium shifts…
Add reactantToward products
Increase pressureToward fewer gas moles
Increase T (endothermic)Toward products
🏭 The Haber process (N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃) exploits this: high pressure and moderate temperature push equilibrium toward ammonia while keeping the rate workable.
🔋 Electrochemistry — Cells & the Nernst Equation REDOX ▶
Chemistry ⇄ Electricity
Redox reactions that do (or need) electrical work
Electrochemistry underlies batteries, fuel cells, corrosion, electroplating and electrolysis. Cell potential links directly to Gibbs energy.
▸ CELL POTENTIAL & NERNST
ΔG = −n F E          E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode

Nernst:  E = E° − (RT/nF) ln Q   = E° − (0.0592/n) log Q  (25 °C)

n = electrons transferred    F = 96 485 C/mol

🧂 Solubility, Ksp & Precipitation EQUILIBRIA ▶
Will It Dissolve or Drop Out?
The solubility product governs precipitation
Whether a salt stays dissolved or precipitates — fouling a heat exchanger, forming a product crystal, or scaling a pipe — is set by the solubility product Ksp.
▸ THE SOLUBILITY PRODUCT
For  A_xB_y(s) ⇌ xA + yB :
  Ksp = [A]^x [B]^y

Ion product Q vs Ksp:
  Q < Ksp → unsaturated (dissolves)
  Q = Ksp → saturated (equilibrium)
  Q > Ksp → supersaturated (precipitates)
▸ WHAT SHIFTS IT
FactorEffect on solubility
Common ionDecreases (adds a product ion)
TemperatureUsually increases (most salts)
pHMatters for weak-acid/base salts
❄️ Controlled supersaturation is exactly how crystallization (Unit Ops) grows product crystals — too fast and you nucleate fines instead of large crystals.

Following the Electrons

Organic synthesis (and much of the chemical industry) runs on a few reaction archetypes. Knowing the mechanism predicts products, rates and selectivity.
▸ THE MAJOR MECHANISMS

🧊 Solutions & Colligative Properties PHYSICAL CHEM ▶
It's the Number, Not the Identity
Properties that depend on how many, not what
Some solution properties depend only on the concentration of dissolved particles, not their chemical identity — the colligative properties behind antifreeze, osmosis and desalination driving forces.
▸ THE FOUR COLLIGATIVE PROPERTIES
PropertyRelation
Boiling-point elevationΔT_b = i·K_b·m
Freezing-point depressionΔT_f = i·K_f·m
Vapor-pressure loweringRaoult: P = x_solvent·P°
Osmotic pressureΠ = i·M·R·T
▸ i, THE VAN'T HOFF FACTOR
i = particles per formula unit (NaCl → i ≈ 2, sugar → 1).
More dissolved particles → bigger colligative effect.
💧 Osmotic pressure sets the energy floor for reverse-osmosis desalination — you must beat Π to push pure water through the membrane (see Mass Transfer).
