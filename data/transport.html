🌐 Navier–Stokes & Dimensionless Groups GOVERNING EQUATIONS ▶
Equations of Change
Conservation of momentum in a fluid
The Navier–Stokes equations are Newton's second law for a continuum fluid. Dimensionless groups compress the physics into ratios that govern scale-up and correlation.
▸ NAVIER–STOKES (incompressible)
ρ(∂v/∂t + v·∇v) = −∇P + μ∇²v + ρg
   inertia          pressure  viscous  body force
▸ KEY DIMENSIONLESS GROUPS
GroupRatioGoverns
Reynolds (Re)inertia / viscousFlow regime
Prandtl (Pr)momentum / thermal diff.Velocity vs. thermal BL
Schmidt (Sc)momentum / mass diff.Velocity vs. conc. BL

🔬 Correlations take the form Nu = f(Re, Pr) and Sh = f(Re, Sc) — the heat/mass-transfer analogy in action.
🧴 Shell Balances — the Falling Film DERIVATION ▶
Building a Velocity Profile from Scratch
The archetypal transport-phenomena problem
A thin liquid film flowing down a wall is the canonical shell-balance exercise: balance momentum on a thin shell, integrate, apply boundary conditions, and out comes the velocity profile.
▸ THE PROCEDURE & RESULT
Momentum shell balance → d(τ)/dx = ρ g cosβ
Integrate + BCs (τ = 0 at surface, v = 0 at wall):

  v_z(x) = (ρ g δ² cosβ / 2μ) [ 1 − (x/δ)² ]   (parabolic)

Max velocity at the free surface; average = (2/3) v_max.
▸ BOUNDARY CONDITIONS TOOLKIT
At a…Condition
Solid wallNo slip: v = 0
Free liquid surfaceNo shear: τ = 0
Fluid-fluid interfacev and τ continuous
🧠 Every shell balance is the same recipe: balance the flux over a thin shell → ODE → integrate → apply BCs. Master it once, reuse it everywhere.
〽️ Boundary-Layer Theory NEAR-WALL ▶
Where the Action Is
Thin near-wall layers carry the gradients
Prandtl's insight: viscous, thermal and concentration effects concentrate in thin boundary layers near a surface, while the bulk flow behaves as if inviscid. This split makes transport problems tractable.
▸ THE THREE BOUNDARY LAYERS

🌪️ Turbulence & Eddy Transport ADVANCED ▶
Chaos That Mixes
Eddies transport far faster than molecules
Above the critical Reynolds number, flow becomes turbulent — chaotic eddies dramatically enhance momentum, heat and mass transport beyond molecular diffusion.
▸ REYNOLDS AVERAGING
Split each quantity into mean + fluctuation:  v = v̄ + v'

Averaging the Navier-Stokes eqns leaves a new term — the
Reynolds stress −ρ⟨v'v'⟩ — modeled with an eddy viscosity.

Total flux = molecular + turbulent (eddy) contribution.
▸ EDDY DIFFUSIVITIES
TransportEffective diffusivity
Momentumν + ε_M (eddy)
Heatα + ε_H
MassD + ε_D
🌀 Turbulence is why stirring speeds mixing so much — eddy diffusivities can dwarf molecular ones by orders of magnitude. It's also the "closure problem" CFD must model.
📐 Convective Transport Correlations DESIGN TOOLS ▶
Turning Groups into Coefficients
Nu = f(Re, Pr) and its mass-transfer twin
Design needs actual h and k_c values. Empirical correlations in dimensionless groups deliver them — the practical payoff of transport analysis.
▸ WORKHORSE CORRELATIONS
Turbulent pipe (heat):  Nu = 0.023 Re^0.8 Pr^0.4   (Dittus-Boelter)
Mass-transfer analog:   Sh = 0.023 Re^0.8 Sc^(1/3)
Flat plate (laminar):   Nu = 0.332 Re^0.5 Pr^(1/3)

Then  h = Nu·k/L  and  k_c = Sh·D/L.
▸ THE CHILTON–COLBURN ANALOGY
j_H = j_D = f/2   (heat & mass transfer factors ≈ friction/2)
→ measure one transport, infer the others.
🔗 The analogy is why a correlation measured for heat transfer can be reused for mass transfer just by swapping Pr → Sc.
🧬 Diffusion with Chemical Reaction COUPLED ▶
Transport and Reaction Together
When molecules react as they diffuse
In catalyst pellets, gas absorption with reaction, and living tissue, diffusion and reaction happen simultaneously. A shell balance now carries a reaction term.
▸ THE GOVERNING BALANCE
Steady 1-D:  D d²C/dx² − k·C = 0   (diffusion − consumption = 0)

Solution involves the Thiele modulus  φ = L√(k/D):
  φ small → reaction-limited (uniform C)
  φ large → diffusion-limited (C drops sharply near the surface)
▸ WHERE IT SHOWS UP
SystemCoupling
Catalyst pelletPore diffusion + surface reaction (effectiveness factor)
Gas absorption + reactionEnhancement of mass transfer
Biofilm / tissueNutrient diffuses in as cells consume it
🔬 The same math (φ, effectiveness factor) reappears in Reaction Engineering and Biochemical — a unifying idea across the curriculum.
🔀 Multicomponent Diffusion (Maxwell–Stefan) ADVANCED ▶
Beyond Fick for Mixtures
When species diffusion is coupled
Fick's law works for dilute binaries. In concentrated multicomponent mixtures, species interactions couple the fluxes — the Maxwell–Stefan framework handles it.
▸ THE MAXWELL–STEFAN VIEW
Driving force (∇ of chemical potential) is balanced by
friction between each pair of species:

  −∇x_i ≈ Σ (x_i x_j / D_ij)(v_i − v_j)

Uses pairwise D_ij; reduces to Fick for a dilute binary.
▸ SURPRISING EFFECTS
PhenomenonMeaning
Reverse diffusionA species moves up its own gradient (dragged by others)
Osmotic diffusionFlux with zero gradient
🔬 Rigorous distillation and membrane models use Maxwell–Stefan because real multicomponent systems can violate simple Fickian intuition.
🔥 The Energy Equation & Viscous Dissipation GOVERNING EQUATION ▶
Heat's Equation of Change
Convection, conduction, and friction heating
Just as Navier–Stokes governs momentum, the energy equation governs the temperature field — including heat generated by the fluid's own internal friction.
▸ THE ENERGY EQUATION (form)
ρc_p (∂T/∂t + v·∇T) = k∇²T + Φ_viscous + q_gen
   accumulation+convection   conduction  friction  sources

Φ_viscous = viscous dissipation (mechanical energy → heat).
▸ WHEN DISSIPATION MATTERS
SituationEffect
High-viscosity flowPolymer melts self-heat (extruders)
High shear / speedBearings, high-speed lubrication
Ordinary flowsUsually negligible
🍯 Viscous dissipation is why polymer extruders can heat their own melt — the shear work becomes significant heat (ties to Non-Newtonian Fluids & Polymer Processing).
🎈 Natural Convection & Buoyancy-Driven Flow FREE CONVECTION ▶
Flow Without a Pump
Density differences do the moving
When fluid near a hot surface expands, becomes lighter and rises, buoyancy drives the flow with no pump or fan. Natural convection sets losses from vessels, electronics and buildings.
▸ THE GOVERNING GROUPS
Grashof  Gr = gβΔT L³/ν²   (buoyancy / viscous)
Rayleigh Ra = Gr·Pr        (drives the correlation)
Nu = C·Ra^n   (n ≈ 1/4 laminar, 1/3 turbulent)
▸ FORCED vs FREE
CompareRegime
Gr/Re² ≪ 1Forced convection dominates
Gr/Re² ≈ 1Mixed — both matter
Gr/Re² ≫ 1Free convection dominates
🔥 Natural-convection coefficients are small — which is exactly why still air is a good insulator and why fins are added to boost the low-h side (see Heat Transfer).
