⚖️ The General Balance Equation FOUNDATION ▶
Conservation Law
In = Out + Generation − Consumption − Accumulation
Every material and energy balance in chemical engineering descends from a single bookkeeping statement applied to a defined control volume. Choose a basis, draw the boundary, then account for everything that crosses it.
▸ THE GENERAL BALANCE
Accumulation = In − Out + Generation − Consumption

Steady state  →  Accumulation = 0
Non-reactive  →  Generation = Consumption = 0
  ⇒ In = Out   (total mass always conserved)
▸ BALANCE TYPES
QuantityConserved?Notes
Total massAlwaysNo generation term (non-nuclear)
Total molesNot alwaysChanges when moles react (A + B → C)
Element / atomAlwaysAtoms are conserved in every reaction
EnergyAlways1st Law; includes heat & work
💡 Strategy: pick the balance that gives the most equations with the fewest unknowns — atom balances shine for combustion, component balances for separations.
🔁 Recycle, Bypass & Purge FLOWSHEET ▶
Multi-Unit Balances
Improving conversion and controlling inerts
Real plants rarely convert feed in one pass. Three flow structures reshape the overall vs. single-pass performance.
▸ THE THREE STRUCTURES
StructurePurposeKey effect
RecycleReturn unreacted feedOverall conversion ≫ single-pass conversion
BypassSplit stream around a unitBlend to hit a target spec
PurgeBleed off a recycle loopPrevents inert / impurity build-up
▸ CONVERSION DEFINITIONS
Single-pass X = (reacted in reactor) / (fed to reactor)
Overall     X = (reacted in reactor) / (fresh feed to process)

With recycle:  Overall X  >  Single-pass X
🧷 A steady recycle loop with a reaction that produces an inert must have a purge, or the inert accumulates without bound.
🔥 Combustion & Degree-of-Freedom Analysis METHOD ▶
Solvability
Counting equations before you solve
A degree-of-freedom (DOF) analysis tells you whether a problem is solvable before algebra begins.
▸ DOF FORMULA
DOF = (unknowns) − (independent balances)
          − (specifications) − (given relations)

DOF = 0  → exactly solvable
DOF > 0  → underspecified (need more info)
DOF < 0  → overspecified (possible inconsistency)
▸ COMBUSTION VOCABULARY
TermMeaning
Theoretical airExact O₂ for complete combustion to CO₂ + H₂O
Excess airAir supplied above theoretical (drives completion)
Wet / dry basisFlue-gas analysis with / without water vapor
Orsat analysisClassic dry flue-gas composition measurement
🎯 Atom balances (C, H, O, N) are the workhorse of combustion problems — one equation per element.
📏 Units, Dimensions & Unit Conversion FOUNDATION ▶
Get the Units Right First
Dimensional homogeneity is non-negotiable
Every valid equation is dimensionally homogeneous — both sides carry the same units. More exam points are lost to unit slips than to any concept. Carry units through every step.
▸ BASE DIMENSIONS & SI UNITS
DimensionSI unitUSCS
Mass Mkglbm
Length Lmft
Time Tss / h
Temperature ΘK°R
Amount Nmollbmol
▸ THE gc CONVERSION FACTOR (USCS)
F = m·a / gc

gc = 32.174 lbm·ft / (lbf·s²)   = 1 kg·m/(N·s²) in SI

Force in lbf needs gc; in SI, 1 N ≡ 1 kg·m/s² (gc = 1).
💡 Convert by multiplying by unity ratios (e.g. 1 = 2.54 cm / 1 in). Weight = m·g; mass ≠ weight — keep lbm and lbf distinct.
🧮 Reactive Balances — Extent of Reaction METHOD ▶
One Variable Ties a Reaction Together
The extent ξ bookkeeps every species at once
For a single reaction, the extent of reaction ξ links the change in every species through its stoichiometric coefficient — collapsing many unknowns into one.
▸ THE EXTENT RELATION
n_i = n_i0 + ν_i · ξ

ν_i < 0 for reactants, > 0 for products.
Molar flow version:  ṅ_i = ṅ_i0 + ν_i · ξ̇
▸ CONVERSION, YIELD & SELECTIVITY
MetricDefinition
Limiting reactantRuns out first (smallest feed/ν ratio)
Conversion X(reacted) / (fed) of limiting reactant
Yield(desired product formed) / (theoretical max)
Selectivity(desired) / (undesired) product
🎯 Atom balances need no ξ and are often faster; extent shines when rate/equilibrium data pin ξ directly.
🔗 Multiple-Unit Balances & Flowsheet Strategy STRATEGY ▶
Divide and Conquer
Where to draw the boundary matters most
A flowsheet is a set of connected control volumes. The art is choosing subsystems that expose solvable balances — often the overall process first, then individual units.
▸ CHOOSING SUBSYSTEMS
BoundaryWhy draw it
Overall processRecycle/internal streams cancel — fewest unknowns
Around a mixing pointIsolates a splitter/blender relation
Single unitNeeded once overall DOF is used up
▸ SOLUTION ORDER
1. Draw & label the flowsheet (all streams, compositions)
2. Pick a basis
3. DOF on the overall system → solve what you can
4. Move to the subsystem with DOF = 0
5. Repeat until every stream is known
🎯 Never write balances for every unit at once — pick the boundary with the fewest unknowns and the equations fall out in sequence.
💦 Psychrometry & Humidity Balances AIR-WATER ▶
Water in Air
The basis for drying, cooling towers & HVAC
Balances on humid air use the humidity ratio — mass of water per mass of dry air — because dry air is the conserved (tie) component.
▸ KEY QUANTITIES
Absolute humidity  H = (18/29) · p_w / (P − p_w)   [kg water / kg dry air]
Relative humidity  RH = p_w / p_w^sat × 100%
Dew point = T at which p_w = p_w^sat (condensation begins)
▸ CHARACTERISTIC TEMPERATURES
TermMeaning

📜 The Laws of Thermodynamics FOUNDATION ▶
First Principles
Energy is conserved; entropy sets the direction
Thermodynamics governs what processes are possible and how much useful work they can deliver. Four laws frame every energy balance a chemical engineer writes.
▸ THE LAWS
LawStatementConsequence
ZerothThermal equilibrium is transitiveTemperature is well-defined
FirstΔU = Q − WEnergy conserved; no perpetual motion (1st kind)
SecondΔSuniv ≥ 0Heat won't spontaneously flow cold→hot
ThirdS → 0 as T → 0 KAbsolute zero is unattainable
▸ OPEN-SYSTEM ENERGY BALANCE (STEADY)
Q̇ − Ẇs = Σ ṁ_out (h + v²/2 + gz)
              − Σ ṁ_in  (h + v²/2 + gz)

Often the KE and PE terms are negligible:
  Q̇ − Ẇs = ṁ Δh
🔑 Enthalpy h = u + Pv bundles internal energy with flow work — it is the natural state variable for open (flow) systems.
🎲 Entropy, Gibbs Energy & Spontaneity 2ND LAW ▶
Direction of Change
ΔG decides whether a process will go
At constant T and P the Gibbs free energy packages the 1st and 2nd laws into one criterion for spontaneity and equilibrium.
▸ GIBBS CRITERION
G = H − TS        dG = V dP − S dT

ΔG < 0  → spontaneous (favorable)
ΔG = 0  → at equilibrium
ΔG > 0  → non-spontaneous (needs work / coupling)

ΔG° = −RT ln K      (reaction equilibrium constant)
▸ ENTHALPY–ENTROPY SIGN MAP
ΔHΔSSpontaneous?
−+Always (all T)
+−Never
−−Only at low T
++Only at high T
♨️ Le Chatelier: raise T on an endothermic reaction (ΔH > 0) and equilibrium shifts toward products — consistent with ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS°.
🫧 Equations of State & Vapor–Liquid Equilibrium PHASE BEHAVIOR ▶
P–V–T & Phase Split
From ideal gas to cubic EOS and Raoult's law
Predicting how a mixture splits between vapor and liquid is the beating heart of separations design.
▸ EQUATIONS OF STATE
ModelForm / use
Ideal gasPV = nRT — low P, high T
Van der WaalsAdds a (attraction) & b (volume)
Peng–RobinsonCubic EOS, excellent for hydrocarbons
Soave–RKCubic EOS, common in process simulators
▸ VLE RELATIONS
Raoult's law (ideal):   y_i P = x_i P_i^sat
Modified Raoult:        y_i P = x_i γ_i P_i^sat
Relative volatility:    α = (y_A/x_A)/(y_B/x_B)

α > 1  → separation by distillation is feasible
α → 1  → separation becomes very difficult
🌡️ An azeotrope (α = 1) can't be broken by ordinary distillation — you need pressure-swing, extractive, or azeotropic distillation.
🎈 Real Gases — the Compressibility Factor Z PVT ▶
When PV = nRT Fails
Z measures the departure from ideality
At high pressure or near condensation the ideal-gas law breaks down. The compressibility factor Z corrects it, and the principle of corresponding states makes Z nearly universal in reduced coordinates.
▸ DEFINITION & REDUCED PROPERTIES
PV = Z n R T          Z = PV / nRT

Reduced:  Tr = T/Tc      Pr = P/Pc
Generalized (Nelson-Obert / Pitzer) charts: Z = Z(Tr, Pr, ω)
▸ READING Z
Z valueMeaning
Z = 1Ideal-gas behavior
Z < 1Attractions dominate (near condensation)
Z > 1Repulsions dominate (very high P)
🌡️ The acentric factor ω captures molecular non-sphericity and feeds both the generalized charts and cubic EOS (SRK, Peng-Robinson).
🔥 Thermochemistry — Heats of Reaction & Formation ENERGY BALANCE ▶
Energy Released or Absorbed
Hess's law turns tabulated data into any ΔH
Reactor energy balances need the heat of reaction. Because enthalpy is a state function, Hess's law lets you build any ΔH from tabulated enthalpies of formation.
▸ CORE RELATIONS
ΔH°rxn = Σ ν_i ΔH°f (products) − Σ ν_i ΔH°f (reactants)

ΔH < 0  → exothermic (releases heat)
ΔH > 0  → endothermic (absorbs heat)

Adiabatic flame temp: all ΔHrxn heats the products (Q = 0).
▸ KEY QUANTITIES
TermMeaning
ΔH°fFormation from elements (elements = 0)
ΔH°cCombustion to CO₂ + H₂O
Sensible heat∫ Cp dT — heating/cooling without reaction
Latent heatΔHvap, ΔHfus — phase change at constant T
♨️ Choose a reference state, compute ΔH along a convenient path (state function!), and the answer is path-independent — the heart of every reactor energy balance.
🔄 Power & Refrigeration Cycles CYCLES ▶
Turning Heat into Work (and Back)
Rankine, Brayton & vapor-compression
Thermodynamic cycles are the engines of the energy industry. Each is a loop of four processes analyzed with the open-system energy balance on each device.
▸ THE MAJOR CYCLES
CycleWorking fluidUse
RankineWater/steamSteam power plants
BraytonAir/gasGas turbines, jet engines
Vapor-compressionRefrigerantRefrigeration, heat pumps
▸ EFFICIENCY & COP
Power cycle:  η = W_net / Q_in       (Carnot limit: 1 − T_C/T_H)
Refrigerator: COP = Q_C / W_in
Heat pump:    COP = Q_H / W_in = COP_refrig + 1
♨️ Reheat and regeneration push the Rankine cycle toward the Carnot limit — more equipment, higher efficiency.
🧩 Fugacity & Activity Coefficients NON-IDEALITY ▶
Real-Mixture Equilibrium
The rigorous criterion behind VLE
Phase equilibrium means equal fugacity of each species in every phase. Fugacity is an "effective pressure"; activity coefficients correct the liquid phase for non-ideal mixing.
▸ THE EQUILIBRIUM CRITERION
f_i^vapor = f_i^liquid    (for every species i)

Vapor:  f_i^V = y_i φ_i P            (φ = fugacity coefficient)
Liquid: f_i^L = x_i γ_i f_i^pure     (γ = activity coefficient)

γ → 1 for ideal solutions (Raoult's law recovered).
▸ ACTIVITY-COEFFICIENT MODELS
ModelBest for
Margules / Van LaarSimple binaries
Wilson / NRTLPolar, non-ideal mixtures
UNIQUAC / UNIFACPredictive (group contribution)
🧪 γ > 1 (positive deviation) points toward a minimum-boiling azeotrope — the usual case for ethanol-water.

🪈 Bernoulli & the Mechanical Energy Balance FOUNDATION ▶
Energy Along a Streamline
Pressure, velocity and elevation trade off
Bernoulli's equation is the frictionless mechanical-energy balance; the engineering (extended) form adds pump work and friction losses.
▸ EXTENDED BERNOULLI (per unit mass)
P₁/ρ + v₁²/2 + g z₁ + w_pump
  = P₂/ρ + v₂²/2 + g z₂ + h_friction

Ideal (no pump, no friction):
  P/ρ + v²/2 + gz = constant
▸ WHAT EACH TERM IS
TermMeaning
P/ρFlow (pressure) energy
v²/2Kinetic energy
gzPotential (elevation) energy
h_frictionIrreversible loss — always positive
💨 Faster flow ⇒ lower static pressure (the Venturi effect) — the physics behind flow meters, aspirators and lift.
🌀 Reynolds Number & Pipe Friction FLOW REGIME ▶
Laminar vs. Turbulent
One dimensionless group predicts the regime
The Reynolds number compares inertial to viscous forces and sets the whole character of the flow — and thus the pressure drop.
▸ REYNOLDS NUMBER & REGIMES
Re = ρ v D / μ = v D / ν

Re < 2100      → laminar   (f = 64/Re)
2100–4000      → transition
Re > 4000      → turbulent (Colebrook / Moody chart)
▸ PRESSURE DROP (DARCY–WEISBACH)
ΔP = f · (L/D) · (ρ v² / 2)

f  = Darcy friction factor (from Re + ε/D)
L  = pipe length      D = inside diameter
ε  = roughness        ε/D = relative roughness
📊 The Moody chart plots f vs. Re for a family of relative-roughness curves — the classic tool for turbulent pipe sizing.
⚙️ Pumps, Head Curves & NPSH EQUIPMENT ▶
Moving Liquids
Operating point & the cavitation limit
A centrifugal pump runs where its head curve crosses the system curve. Too little suction head and the liquid flashes — cavitation.
▸ NPSH — THE CAVITATION GUARD
NPSH_available = (P_suction − P_vapor)/(ρg) + v²/2g − h_losses
NPSH_required  = pump datasheet value

Rule:  NPSH_available > NPSH_required
       (typical margin ≥ 0.5–1 m)  ⇒ no cavitation
▸ AFFINITY LAWS (SPEED N)
QuantityScales as
Flow Q∝ N
Head H∝ N²
Power P∝ N³
⚠️ Cavitation pits impellers and collapses head — chronic low NPSH is a top cause of pump failure in the field.
🌡️ Fluid Statics & Manometry PRESSURE ▶
Pressure in a Fluid at Rest
Depth sets the pressure; manometers read it
A static fluid's pressure rises linearly with depth. This one idea underlies manometers, barometers, buoyancy and the loads on tanks and dams.
▸ HYDROSTATIC LAW & MANOMETRY
P = P₀ + ρ g h            (pressure grows with depth h)

Manometer:  ΔP = (ρ_manometer − ρ_fluid) g Δh
Gauge vs absolute:  P_abs = P_gauge + P_atm
▸ BUOYANCY (ARCHIMEDES)
F_buoyancy = ρ_fluid · g · V_displaced

Floats if ρ_body < ρ_fluid; the displaced weight = body weight.
💧 Pressure at a given depth is the same in all directions and independent of container shape — Pascal's principle.
📟 Flow Measurement INSTRUMENTATION ▶
Reading the Flow Rate
Most meters turn ΔP into velocity
Head-type meters create a constriction; the pressure drop across it reveals the flow rate via a Bernoulli/energy balance and a discharge coefficient C.
▸ ORIFICE / VENTURI EQUATION
Q = C · A₂ · sqrt( 2 ΔP / (ρ (1 − β⁴)) )

β = d/D (bore ratio)      C = discharge coefficient
Orifice C ≈ 0.6 (cheap, high loss) · Venturi C ≈ 0.98 (low loss)
▸ METER TYPES
MeterPrinciple
Orifice plateΔP across a hole — simple, lossy
VenturiSmooth contraction — low permanent loss
RotameterVariable area, float height reads flow
Pitot tubeStagnation vs static pressure → local velocity
💨 Venturi costs more but recovers most of the pressure; the orifice is cheap but its permanent loss shows up on the pump bill.
🚀 Compressible & Choked Flow HIGH SPEED ▶
When Density Changes
The Mach number and the sonic limit
When a gas moves fast or drops a lot of pressure, density is no longer constant. The Mach number Ma = v/c governs the regime, and flow can choke at Ma = 1.
▸ MACH REGIMES & CHOKING
Ma = v / c        c = sqrt(k R T / M)   (speed of sound)

Ma < 0.3  → treat as incompressible
Ma = 1    → sonic / choked at the throat
Choked:  further lowering downstream P does NOT raise flow
▸ CRITICAL PRESSURE RATIO
P*/P₀ = (2/(k+1))^(k/(k−1))   ≈ 0.528 for air (k = 1.4)

Below this ratio → the nozzle/relief is choked.
⚠️ Choked flow sets the maximum discharge through relief valves and restriction orifices — central to relief-system sizing (see Process Safety).
🍯 Non-Newtonian Fluids & Rheology RHEOLOGY ▶
When Viscosity Isn't Constant
Polymers, slurries, foods & blood
Many industrial fluids don't obey Newton's law — their apparent viscosity changes with shear rate. Handling them needs rheology.
▸ FLUID CLASSES
TypeBehaviorExample
Newtonianμ constantWater, air, oils
Shear-thinningμ drops with shearPaint, blood, polymers
Shear-thickeningμ rises with shearCornstarch slurry
Bingham plasticYield stress, then flowsToothpaste, drilling mud
▸ POWER-LAW MODEL
τ = K (dv/dy)^n
  n < 1 shear-thinning · n = 1 Newtonian · n > 1 shear-thickening
🎨 Shear-thinning is exploited on purpose: paint flows under the brush (high shear) then stops dripping at rest (low shear).

🌡️ The Three Modes of Heat Transfer FOUNDATION ▶
Conduction · Convection · Radiation
Every heat path is one (or a mix) of three
Heat always flows from hot to cold. Design amounts to controlling the resistance to that flow — insulating it, or intensifying it.
▸ GOVERNING LAWS
ModeLawDriving factor
Conductionq = −kA dT/dx (Fourier)Thermal conductivity k
Convectionq = hA(T_s − T_∞) (Newton)Film coefficient h
Radiationq = εσA(T₁⁴ − T₂⁴)Emissivity ε, T⁴
▸ THERMAL RESISTANCE NETWORK
q = ΔT / ΣR      (resistances in series add)

Conduction (plane):  R = L / (kA)
Convection:           R = 1 / (hA)
Composite wall:       R_total = ΣR_i
🔗 The resistance analogy (à la Ohm's law) lets you stack walls, films and insulation into one overall U-value.
🔥 Heat Exchangers — LMTD & NTU DESIGN ▶
Sizing & Rating
How much area to transfer a given duty
The shell-and-tube exchanger is the workhorse of the process industries. Two methods size it: LMTD when outlet temps are known, ε-NTU when they are not.
▸ LMTD METHOD
Q = U · A · F · ΔT_lm

ΔT_lm = (ΔT₁ − ΔT₂) / ln(ΔT₁/ΔT₂)

U = overall heat-transfer coefficient
F = correction factor (1.0 for true counter-current)
▸ FLOW ARRANGEMENTS
ArrangementCharacteristic
Counter-currentHighest ΔT_lm; most efficient
Co-currentOutlet temps can only approach, not cross
Cross-flowCommon in air coolers; uses F factor
🧼 Fouling adds a resistance R_f that degrades U over time — designers add a fouling allowance and schedule cleaning.
⏱️ Transient Conduction — Lumped Capacitance & Biot UNSTEADY ▶
How Fast Does It Heat/Cool?
When a body has one uniform temperature
If internal conduction is much faster than surface convection, a body heats or cools uniformly — the lumped-capacitance model, valid when the Biot number is small.
▸ BIOT NUMBER — THE VALIDITY TEST
Bi = h L_c / k          L_c = V / A_surface

Bi < 0.1  → lumped model OK (uniform internal T)
Bi > 0.1  → gradients matter → use Heisler charts / PDE
▸ LUMPED TEMPERATURE RESPONSE
(T − T_∞)/(T₀ − T_∞) = exp(−t / τ)

τ = ρ V c_p / (h A)     (thermal time constant)
Fourier number  Fo = α t / L_c²   (dimensionless time)
🔥 Small Bi means the surface film — not the solid — is the bottleneck. A thin metal part in slow-moving air is the classic lumped case.
🪶 Fins & Extended Surfaces ENHANCEMENT ▶
More Area, More Heat
When convection is the bottleneck, add surface
Fins extend a surface to move more heat when the convective coefficient h is low (gases). A temperature gradient runs along the fin, so not all of it works equally hard.
▸ FIN PERFORMANCE
Fin parameter  m = sqrt(hP / kA_c)
Long-fin heat rate  q = sqrt(h P k A_c) · (T_b − T_∞)

Fin efficiency  η_f = q_actual / q_if_whole_fin_at_T_b
▸ WHEN FINS HELP
ConditionFins worth it?
Low h (gas side)Yes — big gain
High h (boiling)Little benefit
High-k materialBetter (flatter gradient)
🔧 Always fin the side with the lower h — that's the controlling resistance (air-cooled exchangers fin the air side).
☀️ Radiation Exchange & View Factors RADIATION ▶
Heat Without a Medium
Geometry and emissivity set the exchange
Radiation needs no medium and scales with T⁴, so it dominates at high temperature (furnaces, flares). The view factor captures how much of one surface "sees" another.
▸ KEY LAWS
Blackbody emissive power  E_b = σ T⁴     σ = 5.67e-8 W/m²K⁴
Two-surface exchange  Q = σ A₁ F₁₂ (T₁⁴ − T₂⁴)   (black)

View-factor rules:  reciprocity  A₁F₁₂ = A₂F₂₁
                    summation    Σ F_ij = 1
▸ SURFACE PROPERTIES
PropertyMeaning
Emissivity εFraction of blackbody emission (0–1)
Absorptivity αFraction absorbed (α = ε at equilibrium)
Reflectivity ρρ = 1 − α (opaque)
🔥 A radiation shield (low-ε foil) between two surfaces can cut radiant transfer dramatically — the principle behind multilayer insulation.
🫧 Boiling & Condensation PHASE CHANGE ▶
Heat Transfer with Phase Change
Huge coefficients — and a dangerous limit
Boiling and condensation move enormous heat fluxes at small ΔT because latent heat is large. But the boiling curve hides a peak beyond which performance collapses.
▸ THE POOL-BOILING CURVE
ΔT_excess → q":
  Natural convection → NUCLEATE boiling (efficient, bubbles)
  → CRITICAL HEAT FLUX (peak) → TRANSITION → FILM boiling

Past CHF a vapor blanket insulates the surface — q" drops and
the surface temperature can spike (burnout).
▸ CONDENSATION MODES
ModeBehavior
Film (Nusselt)Continuous liquid film — the usual design case
DropwiseDroplets shed — much higher h, hard to sustain
🔥 Reboilers and steam heaters are designed to stay in the nucleate-boiling region — crossing the critical heat flux risks tube burnout.

💠 Molecular Diffusion — Fick's Laws FOUNDATION ▶
Species Move Down Gradients
The mass-transfer analog of Fourier's law
Separations exploit differences in how fast species move between phases. At the molecular scale that motion obeys Fick's laws.
▸ FICK'S LAWS
1st law (steady):   J_A = −D_AB (dC_A/dz)
2nd law (transient): ∂C_A/∂t = D_AB ∂²C_A/∂z²

D_AB = binary diffusivity  (gas ≫ liquid ≫ solid)
Flux driven by a concentration gradient.
▸ THE TRANSPORT ANALOGY
TransportFlux lawDiffusivity
Momentumτ = −μ dv/dyν = μ/ρ
Heatq = −k dT/dyα = k/ρc_p
MassJ = −D dC/dyD_AB
🔬 The three transport diffusivities have identical units (m²/s) — the basis of the Prandtl / Schmidt / Lewis number analogies.
🗼 Distillation & the McCabe–Thiele Method SEPARATION ▶
Stage-by-Stage Separation
Trading reflux for stages
The most important separation in the chemical industry. McCabe–Thiele graphically counts the equilibrium stages a column needs for a target purity.
▸ OPERATING LINES
Rectifying:  y = (R/(R+1)) x + x_D/(R+1)
Stripping:   y = (L̄/V̄) x − (W/V̄) x_W
q-line:      slope = q/(q−1)   (feed condition)

R = reflux ratio (L/D)
▸ LIMITING CASES
CaseMeaning
Total refluxMinimum stages (N_min, Fenske)
Minimum refluxInfinite stages (R_min, Underwood)
Actual designR ≈ 1.2–1.5 × R_min (Gilliland)
⚖️ More reflux ⇒ fewer stages but higher energy. The reflux/stage trade-off is the central economic decision in column design.

Other Separation Routes
When distillation isn't the answer
Heat-sensitive, dilute, or close-boiling systems call for separations driven by solubility, partitioning, or selective permeation.
▸ SEPARATION MENU

🎞️ Two-Film Theory & Overall Coefficients INTERPHASE ▶

Where does the mass-transfer resistance live?

▸ OVERALL vs INDIVIDUAL COEFFICIENTS

📈 Rate Laws & the Arrhenius Equation FOUNDATION ▶
How Fast & Why
Concentration and temperature set the rate
Kinetics answers how fast a reaction proceeds — the dimension thermodynamics ignores. Rate laws are empirical; the Arrhenius form captures the steep temperature dependence.
▸ RATE LAW & ORDER
−r_A = k · C_A^a · C_B^b        order = a + b

Zero order:  C_A = C_A0 − kt
First order: ln(C_A/C_A0) = −kt      (t½ = ln2/k)
Second:      1/C_A − 1/C_A0 = kt
▸ ARRHENIUS TEMPERATURE LAW
k = A · exp(−E_a / R T)

ln k = ln A − (E_a/R)(1/T)   → straight line

A   = pre-exponential (frequency) factor
E_a = activation energy   (↑ E_a ⇒ more T-sensitive)
🌡️ Rule of thumb: many reaction rates roughly double for every +10 °C — a direct consequence of the exponential Arrhenius term.
🛢️ Ideal Reactors — Batch, CSTR & PFR DESIGN ▶

so its average rate is lower ⇒ it needs more volume.

🌡️ Non-Isothermal Reactors & Multiple Steady States ENERGY BALANCE ▶
Heat and Rate Are Coupled
Temperature feeds back on the reaction
Reactions release or absorb heat, and rate depends exponentially on temperature (Arrhenius). Coupling the mole and energy balances can produce multiple steady states and runaway.
▸ CSTR ENERGY BALANCE
Heat generated  G(T) = (−ΔH_rxn) · (−r_A) V   (S-shaped in T)
Heat removed    R(T) = ṁ c_p (T − T_0) + UA(T − T_c)  (linear)

Steady state where G(T) = R(T). Up to 3 intersections
→ low, unstable middle, and high (ignited) steady states.
▸ OPERATING CONCERNS
PhenomenonCause

Adiabatic ΔT(−ΔH)·C_A0 / (ρ c_p) — max temp rise

🧽 Heterogeneous Catalysis — Thiele Modulus & Effectiveness DIFFUSION-REACTION ▶
Does the Pore Interior Get Used?
Reaction vs diffusion inside a catalyst pellet
Inside a porous catalyst, reactant must diffuse in while it reacts. The Thiele modulus compares the two rates; the effectiveness factor says how much of the pellet actually works.
▸ THIELE MODULUS & EFFECTIVENESS
φ = L · sqrt(k / D_eff)      (reaction rate / diffusion rate)

η = (actual rate) / (rate with no diffusion limit)

φ ≪ 1  → η ≈ 1  (reaction-limited, whole pellet used)
φ ≫ 1  → η ≈ 1/φ (diffusion-limited, only the shell works)
🔬 Strong pore-diffusion limitation halves the apparent activation energy and makes rate scale with sqrt(k) — a classic diagnostic of internal diffusion control.
📊 Analyzing Rate Data — Integral & Differential Methods LAB → RATE LAW ▶

〰️ Second-Order Dynamics & Response DYNAMICS ▶
Overshoot & Oscillation
The damping ratio shapes the response
Add inertia (or two first-order lags) and a process becomes second order — capable of overshoot and ringing. The damping ratio ζ decides how it settles.
▸ STANDARD SECOND-ORDER FORM
G(s) = K / (τ²s² + 2ζτs + 1)

τ = natural period      ζ = damping ratio
Overshoot & decay ratio depend only on ζ.
▸ RESPONSE REGIMES

ζ < 1Underdamped — overshoots, oscillates
ζ = 1Critically damped — fastest, no overshoot
ζ > 1Overdamped — sluggish, no overshoot
🎛️ Tuning a loop too aggressively drops ζ toward 0 — the system rings and can go unstable. Most loops target ζ ≈ 0.4–0.7 for a fast, damped response.
📉 Frequency Response & Stability Margins STABILITY ▶
Will the Loop Stay Stable?
Bode plots and the margins that keep you safe
Feed a sinusoid through a process and it comes out shifted and scaled. Tracking gain and phase versus frequency (the Bode plot) reveals how close a loop is to instability.
▸ THE STABILITY CRITERION
Closed loop is unstable if, at the frequency where phase = −180°,
the open-loop gain |GH| ≥ 1.

Gain margin  = 1 / |GH| at phase crossover   (want > ~2, i.e. 6 dB)
Phase margin = 180° + phase at gain crossover (want > ~30–45°)
▸ WHAT ERODES STABILITY
FactorEffect
Higher controller gainFaster but less stable
Dead timeAdds phase lag — very destabilizing
More lags in seriesExtra −90° each — closes on −180°
🎛️ Margins are safety factors for the model: keep a healthy gain and phase margin so plant/model mismatch doesn't tip the loop into oscillation.

⚖️ Chemical Equilibrium & Le Chatelier EQUILIBRIA ▶
How Far Does a Reaction Go?
K sets the destination; Le Chatelier predicts the shift
Reactions reach a dynamic equilibrium where forward and reverse rates match. The equilibrium constant K fixes the position; Le Chatelier's principle predicts how disturbances shift it.
▸ EQUILIBRIUM CONSTANT
For  aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD :
  Kc = [C]^c [D]^d / ([A]^a [B]^b)

ΔG° = −RT ln K
van't Hoff:  d(ln K)/dT = ΔH° / RT²   (T-dependence)
▸ LE CHATELIER — STRESS → SHIFT
DisturbanceEquilibrium shifts…
Add reactantToward products
Increase pressureToward fewer gas moles
Increase T (endothermic)Toward products
🏭 The Haber process (N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃) exploits this: high pressure and moderate temperature push equilibrium toward ammonia while keeping the rate workable.

i = i₀ [ exp(α_a F η / RT) − exp(−α_c F η / RT) ]

i₀ = exchange current density (intrinsic activity)
η  = E − E_eq (overpotential)

Large η → Tafel form:  η = a + b·log|i|   (straight line)

📝 Worked: Flash Separator Material Balance SOLVED ▶
Step-by-Step
Splitting a two-component feed
A worked example of the balance method from the Material Balances domain — draw, basis, DOF, solve.
▸ PROBLEM & SOLUTION
Q: 100 kmol/h of 40% benzene / 60% toluene is flashed.
   Vapor is 70% benzene; liquid is 30% benzene. Find V and L.

1. Basis: F = 100 kmol/h.  Unknowns: V, L (DOF = 0, two balances).
2. Overall:      V + L = 100
3. Benzene:      0.70 V + 0.30 L = 0.40(100) = 40
4. Sub L = 100 − V:
   0.70V + 0.30(100 − V) = 40
   0.40V + 30 = 40  →  V = 25 kmol/h
5. L = 75 kmol/h.

Check benzene: 0.70(25) + 0.30(75) = 17.5 + 22.5 = 40 ✓
🎯 Two unknowns, two independent balances (overall + one component) → exactly solvable. Always verify with the un-used component.
📝 Worked: CSTR Volume for a First-Order Reaction SOLVED ▶
Step-by-Step
Reactor sizing from the design equation
A worked application of the CSTR design equation from Reaction Engineering.
▸ PROBLEM & SOLUTION
Q: Liquid-phase A → B, first order, k = 0.5 min⁻¹.
   Feed 10 L/min, C_A0 = 2 mol/L. Want 80% conversion. Find V.

1. Design eqn:  V = F_A0 X / (−r_A)
2. First order:  −r_A = k C_A = k C_A0 (1 − X)
3. F_A0 = v₀ C_A0 = 10 × 2 = 20 mol/min
4. −r_A at exit = 0.5 × 2 × (1 − 0.8) = 0.2 mol/(L·min)
5. V = (20 × 0.8) / 0.2 = 80 L

Space time τ = V/v₀ = 80/10 = 8 min.
🧪 A CSTR reacts at the low exit concentration everywhere — that's why it needs more volume than a PFR for the same conversion.
📝 Worked: Heat-Exchanger Area from LMTD SOLVED ▶
Step-by-Step
Sizing from duty, U and LMTD
A worked application combining an energy balance with the LMTD method from Heat Transfer.
▸ PROBLEM & SOLUTION
Q: Cool 5 kg/s oil (cp = 2 kJ/kg·K) from 120→60 °C with water
   entering 25 °C, leaving 45 °C, counter-current. U = 400 W/m²K.
   Find the area.

1. Duty:  Q = m cp ΔT = 5 × 2000 × (120−60) = 600,000 W
2. End ΔTs (counter-current):
   ΔT₁ = 120 − 45 = 75 °C      ΔT₂ = 60 − 25 = 35 °C
3. LMTD = (75 − 35)/ln(75/35) = 40/0.762 = 52.5 °C
4. A = Q / (U · LMTD) = 600,000 / (400 × 52.5) = 28.6 m²
♨️ Try it in the Calculators domain: enter ΔT₁ = 75, ΔT₂ = 35 in the LMTD tool → 52.5 °C.

💨 The Psychrometric Chart FOUNDATION ▶
Air + Water in One Diagram
Every drying, cooling & HVAC process lives here
The psychrometric chart plots the state of moist air. Reading it turns humidity balances (from the Material Balances domain) into quick graphical answers.

🏭 Cooling Towers & Air Conditioning APPLIED ▶
Rejecting Heat with Air & Water
Simultaneous heat and mass transfer
Cooling towers and air conditioners are simultaneous heat-and-mass-transfer devices — combining the Heat Transfer and Mass Transfer domains in one unit.
▸ COOLING-TOWER LIMITS
Water is cooled by evaporation into air.
Theoretical limit = the air's wet-bulb temperature.

Approach = T_water,out − T_wet-bulb   (smaller = bigger tower)
Range    = T_water,in − T_water,out
▸ KEY IDEAS
TermMeaning

❄️ Gas Liquefaction Cycles FOUNDATION ▶
Reaching Very Low Temperatures
Cooling gases until they turn to liquid
Cryogenics (below ~−150 °C) liquefies air, natural gas and hydrogen. It leans on the Joule–Thomson effect and clever heat integration from the Thermodynamics domain.
▸ THE CORE CYCLES
CycleIdea
Linde–HampsonCompress → cool → Joule–Thomson throttle → partial liquefy
ClaudeAdds an expander (does work) — more efficient
▸ THE JOULE–THOMSON KEY
Throttling cools a gas only below its inversion temperature.

H₂ and He must be pre-cooled first (their inversion T is very low)
— otherwise throttling warms them.
🌡️ Regenerative heat exchange (cold product cools incoming feed) is essential — without it, liquefaction would be hopelessly energy-intensive.
🧊 Air Separation & Cryogenic Storage APPLIED ▶
Distilling the Atmosphere
Making O₂, N₂ and Ar from air
Cryogenic air separation units (ASUs) liquefy air and distill it into oxygen, nitrogen and argon — distillation (Mass Transfer) at −190 °C.
▸ THE ASU IN BRIEF
Filter & compress air → remove H₂O & CO₂ (they'd freeze)
→ cool to near-liquefaction (regenerative HX)
→ cryogenic distillation columns:
   N₂ (bp −196 °C) overhead · O₂ (bp −183 °C) bottoms · Ar side-draw
▸ STORAGE & SAFETY
ConcernHandling
InsulationVacuum-jacketed / multilayer (Dewar)
Material choiceAustenitic steels stay tough at cryo T
HazardsCold burns, O₂ enrichment, asphyxiation
🔩 Ordinary carbon steel turns brittle in the cold — cryogenic service demands austenitic stainless or aluminium (see the Materials domain).
